## What the License Actually Unlocks

Treat the license as a ladder rather than a single purchase. The early rungs cover regulated-but-legal commerce: water that needs testing and filtering, cigarettes that need visual inspection before resale, hydroponic produce, and appraised rare goods. The advanced rungs open the underground market — the space where margins get serious and where Security starts taking an interest in your inventory.

The exact permit names differ by shop archetype, so don't hunt for one universal unlock label. What matters is the pattern below, which holds across every path into the game.

| Access Level | Typical Goods | Paperwork Demand | Main Threat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry-level permits | Water, produce, basic resale stock | Low — testing and filtering | Liability and spoiled stock |
| Specialty permits | Weapons, chemicals, appraised rares | Medium — inspections, recipes, appraisal skill | Fines, addiction fallout, overpaying |
| Advanced licenses | Contraband, underground suppliers | High — capital plus faction standing | Raids, confiscation, bribery |
| Faction-exclusive deals | Restricted, high-margin inventory | Relationship-driven | Political blowback if you switch sides |

The jump from tier two to tier three is where most players stall. You need cash reserves, you need a supplier who trusts you, and you need a place to hide what you just bought.

Once you hold advanced access, the counter becomes the most dangerous part of the shop. Today's customer could be a Security fisher collecting evidence, a revolutionary looking for leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you are useful. Approve it, refuse it, report it, or look away — someone is always taking notes.

| Customer Type | Tell | Smart Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Security fisher | Asks precise questions about origin | Sell legal stock only, log nothing incriminating |
| Revolutionary | Wants leverage, not product | Trade carefully or refuse outright |

The rogue AI chip subplot matters here. Augs look like everyone else until they don't, and every faction agrees on the policy you are expected to enforce with the gun you were given. Missing the signs is expensive; so is shooting first and being wrong.

Beyond customers, keep three plates spinning: rent (always rising), faction standing (always shifting), and inventory concealment (always one inspection away from disaster). Expanding into multiple businesses and new production lines is only worth it if every purchase earns its keep.
