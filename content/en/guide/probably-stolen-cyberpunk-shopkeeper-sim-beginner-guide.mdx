## Choosing a Starting Trade: Match the Trouble to Your Temperament

The game lets you open as a gunsmith, chemist, water merchant, fence, farmer, or from absolute rock bottom. The store page frames this perfectly: you are not picking a theme, you are picking which kind of trouble you want to monetize. Each product line has its own profit curve and its own failure mode.

| Starting Trade | Profit Potential | Primary Risk | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water merchant | Steady, low ceiling | Liability — water must be tested and filtered | Players who want predictable early income |
| Farmer / hydroponics | Slow build, reliable | Time investment, limited early cash | Patient players building a supply base |
| Chemist | High | Addiction fallout and guarded recipes | Players comfortable with recipe management |
| Gunsmith | High | Inspections and permit requirements | Players who like paperwork and precision |
| Fence | Very high | Raids and Security attention | Risk-takers who enjoy hiding stock |
| Rock bottom | Variable | Almost no starting capital | Veterans wanting a hard-mode feel |

### The Safest Opening for a True Beginner

Water is the classic low-variance start. Demand is constant, the product is easy to understand, and the main skill you practice — testing and filtering before resale — transfers directly to every other trade. You will not get rich fast, but you will learn the appraisal rhythm without gambling your rent money.

### The Fastest Opening if You Accept the Risk

Fencing and chemistry produce the biggest margins, but they also invite the biggest consequences. If you take this route, budget a portion of every sale as "bribe money" rather than treating it as profit. Community reports consistently suggest that players who plan for a Security encounter handle it far better than those who assume they will never get caught.

## Your First Week: A Step-by-Step Priority List

This is the part of the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim beginner guide that most players skip, and it is the part that saves runs. Do not chase the highest-margin item on day one. Chase stability, then upgrade.

| Phase | What to Do | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening hours | Stock one cheap, reliable product line and sell it consistently | Builds a cash buffer before rent hits |
| First restock | Inspect every incoming item before paying | Supplier overstatement is a built-in trap |
| Mid-week | Learn one production machine or recipe end to end | Machines turn raw stock into higher-margin artifacts |
| Before rent | Keep a reserve equal to your next payment | Confiscated stock can wipe an unprotected run |
| End of week | Reinvest surplus into a second product line | Diversification softens a bad trade |

A useful mental rule: every purchase has to earn its keep. If an item cannot plausibly return its cost plus a margin within the current rent cycle, it is a luxury, not inventory. Beginners routinely fill shelves with impressive-looking goods that never sell because the customer base for them is thin.

## Appraisal, Haggling, and Pricing: Where Beginners Bleed Money

Most early losses in Probably Stolen come from misjudging what is actually in front of you. Suppliers overstate value, customers push their luck, and contraband often arrives with problems you only discover after the deal closes. Your margin is a direct function of how well you assess both the item and the person selling it.

| Item Type | Required Check | What Goes Wrong if You Skip It |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection | Damaged stock sold as premium |
| Water | Testing and filtering | Liability claims |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | Inspection failures, fines |
| Chemicals | Closely guarded recipes | Wasted materials, addiction fallout |
| Rare goods | Formal appraisal | Overpaying on the buy side |

Haggling is tempting but not free. Every bargain carries a trade-off — a customer who feels squeezed may remember it, and reputation is a currency that opens doors and paints targets at the same time. In practice, the best beginner policy is to haggle on low-stakes items and accept fair offers on high-volume ones so your reputation stays healthy.

You can also cut product quality, relabel counterfeit goods as genuine, or fence discounted stolen merchandise. All three raise margins. All three invite mob justice or a Security visit. If you try them, do it with stock you can afford to lose.

## Factions, Contraband, and Staying Off Security's Radar

Reputation buys access and protection, but it also makes you visible. Building connections with the right factions unlocks exclusive deals and advanced licenses for the underground market, so faction work is not optional flavor — it is an income multiplier. The trade-off is that loyalty to one group can close doors with another.

Contraband management is a mini-game of its own. You need hiding spots, a straight face, and a contingency plan. If Security does find something, bribing your way out is often cheaper than losing the entire shipment — assuming you kept the cash on hand.

There is also the augmentation problem. A rogue sentient AI chip has spread through hosts who look like ordinary customers until they do not, and every faction agrees on the policy: you are expected to spot the signs and act. Missing the cues, or misreading them, carries its own price. Slow down on suspicious customers and watch for behavioral tells rather than guessing.

## Five Beginner Mistakes That Cost You Rent

- **Spending the rent reserve on stock.** Confiscation and bad deals are random enough that an unprotected buffer ends runs.
- **Ignoring machine upgrades.** Production chains are how you escape thin retail margins.
- **Selling to every customer.** Some buyers are security fishers or people testing your usefulness.
- **Over-haggling.** Reputation damage compounds faster than the extra credits help.
- **Skipping licenses.** Underground market access is gated behind progression, not luck.

## FAQ

**Is Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim beginner friendly?**
Yes, with one caveat. The controls are simple and the game is playable with a mouse only, but the economy punishes careless spending. Follow this Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim beginner guide's rent-reserve rule and the difficulty curve feels fair.

**Which starting profession should a new player pick?**
Water merchant or farmer for a first run. Both teach testing, filtering, and supply basics with limited downside. Save gunsmith and fence runs for when you understand the inspection and raid systems.

**How do I stop rising rent from ending my run?**
Keep a reserve equal to your next payment at all times, diversify into a second product line early, and reinvest in machines so your margins grow faster than the rent does.
