## What Is Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim?

The Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Steam release puts you behind a counter on a dystopian space station where every transaction is a small act of gambling. You buy low, appraise carefully, haggle hard, and decide whether the customer in front of you is a paying mark, a security informant, or someone who will get you raided by dinner.

That tension is the whole pitch. You are not running a cozy storefront — you are running a business with rising rent, confiscation risk, and factions who remember what you sold them. The goal is simple to state and hard to survive: earn enough to buy out your shop before the landlord squeezes you out.

If you are still deciding whether to jump in, the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Steam page is the fastest way to check current pricing, system requirements, and the bundle options. This guide covers what actually matters once you are inside the shop.

### The Core Loop in One Paragraph

Buy or craft stock → inspect it for defects and forgeries → price it against the customer in front of you → absorb the consequences. Consequences come in flavors: inspections for weapons, addiction complaints for chemicals, liability for water, and raids for contraband. Every profitable product line is attached to a specific way of ruining your week, and picking a trade is really picking which risk you can manage.

## Choosing Your Starting Trade

Your opening choice shapes the first ten hours more than any other decision. Each trade has its own profit curve and its own failure mode, so pick the one whose punishment you can live with.

| Starting Trade | What You Sell | Primary Risk | Good For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gunsmith | Weapons and mods | Permit inspections and audits | Players who like paperwork and precision |
| Chemist | Profitable chemicals | Addiction fallout and recipe theft | High-margin risk takers |
| Water Merchant | Filtered, tested water | Liability claims | Steady, low-drama income |
| Fence | Discounted stolen goods | Raids and mob justice | Players comfortable with heat |
| Hydroponic Farmer | Grown produce | Slow ramp, machine upkeep | Long-game builders |
| Rock Bottom | Whatever you can afford | Everything | Veterans and masochists |

A practical rule from player experience: your first trade should be the boring one. Water and farming teach you the appraisal and machine-management systems without stacking a raid timer on top of your learning curve. Once rent is comfortably covered, branch into the high-margin, high-heat lines.

Community reports from the first 48 hours consistently flag the same beginner mistake — over-investing in inventory before the first rent cycle is secured. Cash on hand is not wasted money; it is your insurance policy against a bad deal.

## Appraisal, Counterfeits, and Contraband: Where the Margins Live

This is the heart of the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Steam experience, and it is where most players either get rich or get raided. Every item type demands a different verification step before resale.

| Item Type | Required Step | If You Skip It |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection | Counterfeit or damaged stock sold at a loss |
| Water | Test and filter | Liability complaints, reputation damage |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | Inspection failure, confiscation |
| Chemicals | Guarded recipes and dosing | Addiction incidents, faction anger |
| Rare goods | Proper appraisal | You overpay and eat the difference |

Three levers push your margins upward, and all three carry a cost:

- **Quality cutting.** Cheaper inputs, thinner profits per unit, but angry customers who talk.
- **Relabeling.** Passing counterfeit goods off as genuine works beautifully on arrogant elites — right up until someone with leverage notices.
- **Fencing stolen stock.** The best margins in the game, priced against the odds that Security never connects the item to you.

Suppliers inflate value, customers push their luck, and contraband frequently arrives with problems you only spot after the credits change hands. Treat every purchase as an appraisal exam, not a shopping trip. If you cannot identify what is in front of you, walk away — the loss from a missed sale is smaller than the loss from a misjudged buy.

## Customers, Factions, and the Aug Threat

Reputation is the game's real currency. It buys access to exclusive deals and a measure of protection, but it also puts a target on your back. Selling to the wrong person once is survivable; selling to them consistently makes you a name.

| Customer Type | What They Want | Hidden Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Security fisher | Evidence and leverage | Reporting you or setting a trap |
| Revolutionary | Weapons, chemicals, leverage | Pulling you into faction conflict |
| Fallen elite | Counterfeits and discretion | Retaliation if embarrassed |
| Ordinary station resident | Affordable basics | Low risk, low margin |
| Aug (infected host) | Anything, convincingly | Misreading the signs has a price |

That last row deserves its own warning. A sentient AI chip went rogue and spread through hosts, and augmented citizens look ordinary until they very much do not. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you are the one holding the enforcement tool. Miss the signs, or misread an innocent customer, and you pay either way — so slow down on suspicious transactions and watch for behavioral tells rather than cosmetic ones.

Approving, refusing, reporting, or quietly looking away are all valid plays. Just remember that somebody on that station is always taking notes.

## Rent, Expansion, and How to Buy Out Your Shop

Rent is the clock the entire game runs on. Miss it and you lose leverage; pay it comfortably and you unlock expansion, additional businesses, and deeper production chains.

| Phase | Main Goal | Priority Purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First cycle | Cover rent with cash left over | Cheap, fast-moving stock |
| Second cycle | Stabilize one product line | Appraisal tools and storage |
| Mid game | Add a second revenue stream | Machines and production chains |
| Late game | Buy out the shop | Licenses and faction connections |

A few habits that separate a stable shop from a struggling one:

1. **Keep a rent buffer.** Never let a single bad deal determine whether you make payment.
2. **Diversify before you scale.** Confiscated stock hurts far less when it is one line out of three.
3. **Invest in production, not just resale.** Machines and crafting chains convert cheap inputs into artifacts worth several times the material cost.
4. **Unlock advanced licenses early.** Underground market access is where the genuinely profitable inventory lives.
5. **Bribe when the math works.** Losing a shipment stings; losing your license hurts more.

Every purchase should earn its keep. If a new product line cannot pay back its cost within a couple of cycles, it is a hobby, not a business.
