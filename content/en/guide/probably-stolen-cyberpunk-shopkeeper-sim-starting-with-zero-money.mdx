## Why Starting With Zero Money Teaches You the Station's Economy

When you begin with equipment, permits, or a stocked shelf, you can brute-force your way through early mistakes. A rock-bottom start removes that cushion. You feel the cost of a bad appraisal immediately, because you can't absorb it.

The core loop of Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim is simple: buy low, verify what you're actually holding, sell high, and keep the landlord off your back. A zero-money run compresses that loop into something tight and unforgiving. Every decision has three questions attached to it:

- **Can I afford to be wrong about this item?** If not, walk away.
- **Does this sale raise my reputation or paint a target on me?** Both can happen at once.
- **Will this purchase still matter when rent comes due?** If not, it's a luxury.

Players who start from absolute rock bottom consistently report that the first two shifts feel brutal and the third feels like a different game — because by then you've internalized the appraisal and haggling rhythms. Treat the early grind as tuition, not punishment.

One more thing worth knowing before you load in: the game rewards **reputation as currency**. It buys access and protection, but it also makes you visible. On this station, being nobody is a genuine tactical advantage in the first hours.

## Picking a Zero-Capital Product Line

The game lets you open as a gunsmith, chemist, water merchant, fence, farmer, or from absolute rock bottom. Each product line has its own profit curve and its own flavor of catastrophe — weapons draw inspections, chemicals draw addiction problems, water draws liability, and contraband draws raids.

For a zero-money start, you want the lowest barrier to entry and the fastest turnaround. Here's how the archetypes compare on that axis:

| Starting path | Upfront capital | Regulatory risk | Turnaround speed | Zero-money verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water merchant | Very low | Low–moderate (testing, liability) | Fast | Best overall entry point |
| Fence | Low | High (stolen goods, raids) | Fast | Strong profit, high exposure |
| Farmer / hydroponics | Low–moderate | Low | Slow | Good long-term, poor first day |
| Chemist | Moderate | High (recipes, addiction) | Medium | Not recommended at zero |
| Gunsmith | High | Very high (permits, inspections) | Slow | Save for a later run |
| Absolute rock bottom | None | Variable | Fast | The purist challenge |

Community reports suggest water is the friendliest first step because the product is consumable, repeat customers are common, and the inspection routine (test, filter, then sell) is easy to learn. Fencing is the higher-ceiling option if you're confident in your read on both items and buyers.

Whichever you pick, resist the urge to diversify on day one. A zero-money run in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim is won by going narrow, going deep, and only expanding once rent is comfortably covered.

## The First Session: A Step-by-Step Zero-Money Routine

The temptation in the opening hour is to chase every deal on the counter. Don't. Follow a repeatable routine instead, so your decisions stay disciplined when your credit balance is embarrassing.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scout supplier offers before buying anything | Lets you compare stated value against your own read |
| 2 | Appraise every rare or unusual item | Overpaying once can end a zero-money run |
| 3 | Buy only what you can verify cheaply | Inspection costs scale with product type |
| 4 | Set prices slightly above cost, then haggle | Leaves room to "lose" a negotiation gracefully |
| 5 | Track which customers return | Repeat buyers are the backbone of early cash flow |
| 6 | Bank a rent buffer before any upgrade | Confiscated stock or a bad deal can wipe a thin margin |
| 7 | End the shift by reviewing what you misjudged | The appraisal log is your real progress bar |

Two habits separate smooth runs from short ones. First, **never buy an item you can't inspect** with your current tools and licenses — an unverifiable purchase is a coin flip, and coin flips don't compound. Second, **haggle on everything, but don't win everything**. Winning too aggressively burns goodwill you'll need later, and some players report that pushing too hard on the wrong customer invites retaliation.

If a deal feels too good, it usually is. Suppliers overstate value, and contraband frequently arrives with problems you only discover after the credits have changed hands.

## Appraisal, Customers, and Risk Control

### Item Inspection Cheat Sheet

Every product line carries its own verification ritual. Skipping one is how margins quietly disappear.

| Item type | Required step | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection before resale | Assuming sealed means genuine |
| Water | Test and filter before selling | Skipping filtration to save time |
| Weapons | Permits plus required modifications | Selling an unmodified unit into an inspection |
| Chemicals | Follow closely guarded recipes | Improvising and tanking quality |
| Rare goods | Proper appraisal before purchase | Trusting the supplier's stated value |

Beyond inspection, you have levers that boost margin at a cost: cutting product quality, relabeling counterfeits as genuine, or fencing discounted stolen goods. Each is a bet that Security won't reach you. The elite of this station are described by the developers as arrogant and easy to fleece with counterfeit goods — that's real money, but it's also real risk.

### Risk vs. Reward Decisions

| Action | Upside | Downside | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relabeling counterfeits | Large margin spike | Reputation damage, mob justice | Target only low-information buyers |
| Hiding contraband | Access to underground prices | Raids, confiscation | Keep a rent buffer and a hiding spot |
| Bribing an official | Avoids a bust | Recurring cost, leverage over you | Budget it as a fixed expense |
| Reporting a suspicious customer | Faction goodwill | Lost sale, possible retaliation | Do it early, when you're nobody |

That last row matters more than it looks. A sentient AI chip went rogue and took its hosts with it, and augmented citizens look ordinary until they don't. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you're the one holding the gun that enforces it. Missing the signs — or misreading them — carries its own price. Learn the tells early and you'll avoid the worst outcomes.

## Scaling From Zero to a Shop Buyout

The win condition is baked into the premise: earn enough to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand. Rent keeps climbing, so surplus sitting idle is a slow loss. Once you're stable, spend in this order:

| Priority | Where the credits go | Payoff |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rent buffer (several cycles) | Prevents a confiscation spiral |
| 2 | Inspection and appraisal tools | Fewer bad purchases, better margins |
| 3 | Production machines and chains | Turns raw inputs into high-value artifacts |
| 4 | Advanced licenses | Unlocks the underground market |
| 5 | Faction relationships | Exclusive deals and protection |
| 6 | Additional businesses | Diversified income and expansion |

Machines deserve special attention. Production chains convert cheap inputs into artifacts worth far more than the sum of their parts, which is how a zero-capital start eventually outpaces players who began with gear. Faction politics gate the best deals, so cultivate relationships deliberately rather than opportunistically — and remember that every faction you court is a faction that now expects things from you.

## FAQ

**Is starting with zero money in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim viable for a first playthrough?**
Yes, though it's demanding. You'll learn appraisal and haggling fast because you can't absorb mistakes, but many players prefer one guided run first and then a rock-bottom challenge run once they understand the station's factions.

**Which product line is best when you have no capital?**
Water is the safest entry: low upfront cost, fast turnaround, and a simple test-and-filter inspection routine. Fencing offers better margins if you're confident in your read on both items and buyers, but it draws far more attention from Security.

**How do I stop Security from confiscating my stock?**
Hide contraband properly, keep a rent buffer so a single bust doesn't spiral, and treat bribes as a recurring expense rather than a one-time fix. Reporting the occasional suspicious customer early also builds goodwill you can spend later.

**What should I do with my first real profit in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim?**
Bank a multi-cycle rent buffer before anything else. After that, invest in inspection tools, then machines and production chains, and only then chase licenses and faction deals. Idle credits lose value as rent climbs.
