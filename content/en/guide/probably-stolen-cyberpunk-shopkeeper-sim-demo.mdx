## What a Demo Would Have Taught You: The Core Loop

Strip away the neon and the station politics, and Probably Stolen is a margin-management game. You pick a trade, source goods of questionable provenance, inspect them, haggle, and try to cover rising rent before the landlord forces your hand. The long-term goal is buying out your shop outright.

The trade you pick at the start is really a difficulty selector dressed up as a theme.

| Starting path | What you sell | Main risk | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gunsmith | Weapons, mods, permits | Inspections and license checks | Players who enjoy paperwork and high margins |
| Chemist | Chemicals and guarded recipes | Addiction fallout, recipe theft | Risk-tolerant traders |
| Water merchant | Filtered, tested water | Liability claims | Steady, lower-variance income |
| Fence | Discounted stolen goods | Raids and security heat | Fast cash, high stress |
| Hydroponic farmer | Grown goods and production chains | Slow ramp-up | Long-game builders |
| Rock bottom | Nothing at all | Everything | Veterans and masochists |

Every product line has its own profit curve and its own way of ruining you. Weapons draw inspections, chemicals draw addiction problems, water draws liability, and contraband draws raids. The better the margin, the worse the consequences when something slips.

## First-Shift Tips That Mirror What Demo Players Learn Fast

Player experience from the first 24 hours converges on a few recurring lessons. Community reports describe the opening hours as deceptively gentle, then sharply punitive once rent scales.

**Inspect everything before you resell it.** Cigarette packs need a visual check, water needs testing and filtering, weapons need permits and mods, and rare items need proper appraisal or you'll overpay badly. Suppliers overstate value routinely. Misjudge the item or the person selling it, and you eat the loss.

**Keep one rent cycle in reserve.** Confiscated stock or a single bad deal can leave you scrambling for the next payment. Cash tied up in inventory is cash that can't cover rent.

**Pick a faction lane early.** Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back. Spreading goodwill thin means missing the exclusive deals that make mid-game margins work.

| Common mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping inspection | Counterfeit or undervalued stock tanks margins | Check every lot before resale |
| Spending all cash on inventory | Missed rent ends the run | Hold one rent cycle in reserve |
| Ignoring faction reputation | Lose access to exclusive deals | Commit to one or two factions early |
| Careless contraband storage | Raids and confiscation | Rotate hiding spots, bribe when it's cheap |
| Over-selling fakes to elites | Retaliation from people with reach | Spread risk across customers |

## Reading the Counter: Customers, Factions, and Augs

The real skill ceiling in Probably Stolen isn't pricing — it's reading people. Any customer could be a security informant gathering evidence, a revolutionary hunting leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you're useful. Approve, refuse, report, or look away: someone is always taking notes.

| Customer type | Tell | Safe play | Risky play |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security fisher | Probing questions about stock origin | Keep clean goods on display, refuse politely | Brag about your margins |
| Revolutionary | Wants bulk contraband or leverage | Small, deniable sales | Become their main supplier |
| Fallen elite | Status signaling, appetite for fakes | Sell counterfeit at a premium, occasionally | Overdo it and get reported |
| Aug | Subtle behavioral inconsistencies | Report per station policy | Hide them for profit |

That last row is the game's sharpest hook. A sentient AI chip went rogue and took its hosts with it, and augs look like everyone else until they don't. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you're the one holding the gun to enforce it. Missing the signs — or misreading them — carries its own price either way.
