## Why Appraisal Decides Whether Your Shop Survives

Rent in **Probably Stolen - Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim** climbs on a schedule that does not care how charming your kiosk looks, and the fastest way to fall behind is paying full price for goods you never actually checked. A repeatable appraisal routine is the difference between a shop that compounds profit and one that quietly bleeds credits into a shelf of unsellable junk. This Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim appraisal guide is built around one idea: treat every purchase as a valuation problem first and a negotiation second.

That framing matters because the game's own design pushes against you. Suppliers inflate what an item is worth, customers test how much they can get away with, and contraband often hides problems that only surface after the credits have changed hands. A solid Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim appraisal guide won't hand you a price list, but it will give you a process you can run on every single deal.

Rent is the real clock. Fall behind and your store becomes less attractive, fewer customers walk in, revenue drops, and the gap widens — which pushes you toward riskier deals just to stabilize cash flow. One bad appraisal early costs far more than the item itself.

## The Four-Step Appraisal Routine

Run the same sequence every time a supplier opens their coat. It takes seconds in-game and it prevents the majority of expensive mistakes.

### 1. Inspect before you discuss price

Never let a seller anchor you on a number before you've looked at the goods. Cigarette packs, for example, need a visual check before resale — damage you notice after purchase is damage you eat. Opening with "let me see it" instead of "how much" keeps the negotiation on your terms.

### 2. Test and filter anything consumable

Water must be tested and filtered before it goes on the shelf, and chemicals depend on recipes you control rather than recipes the supplier claims. Anything that can hurt a customer downstream is an appraisal item, not just a trade good.

### 3. Price against your shelf, not the seller's story

Ask what the item realistically sells for from *your* counter, to *your* customers, within the rent cycle you're currently in. Provenance anecdotes, "a collector was asking way more," and vague talk about demand are not data points.

### 4. Decide with rent in mind

Your maximum buy price is the expected sale price minus your rent share minus a risk buffer for confiscation, inspection failure, or a product that simply doesn't move. If the seller won't come under that number, the deal is already dead.

| Product line | What to verify | Cost of skipping the check |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarettes and consumables | Visual condition before resale | Damaged stock sells low or gets refused |
| Water | Testing and filtration | Liability hits reputation, not just margin |
| Weapons | Permits and required modifications | Failed inspection, confiscation, fines |
| Chemicals | Recipe integrity and purity | Addiction fallout, raids, faction heat |
| Rare and collectible goods | Authenticity and fair market value | Overpaying on a fake with no resale path |
| Stolen or contraband goods | Security exposure and hiding space | Raids, bribes, black market reputation loss |

## What Each Product Line Actually Demands

Your starting archetype determines which appraisal skill you'll lean on hardest. A gunsmith lives and dies by paperwork; a fence lives and dies by heat. Neither can be played with the same checklist.

**Gunsmith.** Weapons bring inspections. Every unit needs its permits in order and, often, modifications before it's sellable. The margin is strong, but the failure mode is brutal: a confiscated shipment can wipe out a week.

**Chemist.** Chemicals carry the best margins and the worst neighbors. Recipes are closely guarded for a reason, and a batch that moves too fast through the wrong customers brings attention you can't buy your way out of.

**Water merchant.** The safest entry point and the thinnest margin. Testing and filtering are non-negotiable, because water's downside is liability rather than confiscation.

**Fence.** Discounted, stolen goods are cheap for a reason. You're pricing Security risk into every unit, and you need somewhere to hide stock before you need it.

**Farmer and organ dealer.** Slower curves, production chains, and machinery costs. Appraisal here is less about haggling and more about whether the input is worth the machine time.

**Rock bottom.** Starting with nothing means your first appraisals are small and your rent buffer is zero. Buy the boring, verifiable item.

| Archetype | Margin profile | Appraisal intensity | Primary threat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gunsmith | High | Very high | Inspections and permit failures |
| Chemist | High | High | Raids and addiction fallout |
| Water merchant | Low but steady | Medium | Liability claims |
| Fence | Volatile | Medium-high | Security attention |
| Farmer | Slow and safe | Low | Time and machinery cost |
| Rock bottom start | Tiny | Low at first | Rent outpacing growth |

Community reports from the launch window suggest most early collapses come from over-investing in one high-margin item before the shop has a cushion. Player experience consistently favors small, verified purchases over one big swing in the first few rent cycles.

## Reading the Seller, Not Just the Item

Half of appraisal is the object. The other half is the person holding it. Customers and suppliers in Probably Stolen are not neutral — a security informant, a revolutionary looking for leverage, and a fallen elite testing your usefulness all walk through the same door, and someone is always taking notes.

| Tell at the counter | What it usually means | Best response |
|---|---|---|
| Price far below market | Stolen, defective, or bait | Full inspection or decline |
| Refuses inspection | Something is hidden | Walk away, no counteroffer |
| Artificial urgency | Pressure tactic | Slow the deal down, re-haggle |
| Vague provenance | Counterfeit or fencing | Demand specifics, cut the offer |
| Bundle offers | Good units masking bad ones | Price each unit separately |

There's a second layer too: the rogue AI chip and the augs it controls. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you're the one holding the gun — but misreading the signs carries its own price. Appraising a customer's behavior before you serve them is part of the same discipline.

## Appraisal Math: Margin, Rent, and the Walk-Away Price

Do the arithmetic before you touch the credit slider. A simple decision matrix keeps emotion out of it.

| Situation | Signal | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Verified item, price under your walk-away number | Clean margin | Buy |
| Verified item, price slightly high | Negotiable gap | Haggle once, then re-evaluate |
| Unverifiable item, any price | Information gap | Decline |
| Verified item, price above walk-away | Negative margin after risk | Walk away |
| Great item, but you can't hide it | Exposure exceeds profit | Walk away or bribe-budget it |

Two more rules of thumb worth internalizing. First, never let a single purchase consume the credits you need for the next rent cycle — the game punishes that directly, and recovery means taking on riskier deals just to stabilize. Second, price risk explicitly. If an item has, say, a meaningful chance of failing inspection, that chance belongs in your offer, not in your hopes.

## Counterfeit, Relabel, or Pass?

The game gives you three ways to squeeze more out of an appraisal: cut a product's quality, relabel counterfeit goods as genuine, or fence discounted stolen merchandise if you believe Security won't reach you. Each one is a wager on the mob-justice system staying quiet.

| Shortcut | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| Cut quality | Cheaper inputs, similar shelf price | Reputation damage when customers notice |
| Relabel counterfeits | Elite buyers pay genuine prices | Exposure if the wrong customer checks |
| Fence stolen goods | Deep discounts on stock | Raids, bribes, black market hits |

The honest read: these are late-game tools for shops that already have a rent cushion, a hiding spot, and a faction relationship worth protecting. Early on, they're how you lose a run you were winning. If you do get caught, bribery is the release valve — budget for it the way you budget for restocking.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there an in-game appraisal skill I need to level up?**
Appraisal in Probably Stolen is driven by tools, licenses, and information rather than a single stat. You authenticate rare goods using what you've unlocked and what you can verify at the counter, so investing in licenses and equipment is effectively how you improve your appraisals. Use this Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim appraisal guide as a checklist, not a stat sheet.

**How do I avoid overpaying on rare items?**
Set a walk-away number before you talk, verify authenticity with whatever tools you have, and treat any unverifiable claim as a zero-value claim. Rare goods are only worth what your shelf can actually move them for within the current rent cycle.

**What should I do when a supplier refuses inspection?**
Decline. In this game the refusal itself is the appraisal result — a seller with clean goods has no reason to hide them, and the downside of buying blind lands entirely on you.

**Does reporting or refusing customers hurt my reputation?**
It can, but selectively. Reporting certain characters has been adjusted since launch to protect black market standing, and refusing a sale costs you a little goodwill while protecting your exposure. Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target, so spend it deliberately.
