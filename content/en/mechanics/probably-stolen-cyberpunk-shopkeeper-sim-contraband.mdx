## Why Contraband Decides Whether You Keep the Shop

The back room is worth more than the storefront, and that is exactly the problem in **Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim**. Contraband is the fastest route to real margins on the station — and the fastest route to confiscation, raids, and a rent payment you suddenly cannot cover. Learning how smuggled goods move through your inventory is the difference between buying out your shop and losing it to rising rent.

The core loop of **Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim contraband** play is simple to state and brutal to execute: buy low from suppliers who lie about value, verify what you can, hide what you cannot, and sell to customers who may be recording everything. Every product line has a profit curve and a matching way of ruining you. Weapons bring inspections, chemicals bring addiction, water brings liability, and contraband brings raids.

That tension is why the game landed so hard at launch. According to the developer's own launch announcement on the [official Probably Stolen Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4348910/Probably_Stolen_Cyberpunk_Shopkeeper_Sim/), the game moved 200,000 copies within its first few hours and peaked above 86,000 concurrent players. A lot of shopkeepers are learning the contraband rules the hard way right now.

## How Contraband Actually Enters Your Shop

Contraband does not appear from nowhere. It arrives through three doors: discounted stolen goods offered by fences and shady suppliers, counterfeit product you relabel as genuine, and underground market stock unlocked through advanced licenses. Each door has a different paper trail, and the paper trail is what Security follows.

The important mental shift is that contraband is not a separate minigame bolted onto shopkeeping. It is the same buy-inspect-price-sell loop with the risk dial turned up. You still appraise items, still haggle, still manage inventory space. What changes is the consequence column.

- **Suppliers overstate value.** Misjudge an item or the person selling it and you absorb the loss.
- **Defects surface late.** Contraband often comes with issues you only notice after the deal is closed.
- **Margins are not free.** The better the margin, the worse the outcome when something goes wrong.
- **Stock can vanish.** Confiscated inventory is gone, not discounted.

Community reports from early players consistently describe the same rookie mistake: filling the shelf with high-margin stolen goods before building any reputation buffer, then eating a raid with no cash reserve for rent.

## The Contraband Risk Ladder: What Each Product Line Costs You

Not all questionable goods are created equal. The game frames starting professions as a choice of "which kind of trouble you're willing to monetize," and that framing holds for the whole run. Use this table to pick your poison deliberately rather than by accident.

| Product line | Primary verification step | What goes wrong | Heat level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydroponic farmer | Quality and yield checks | Thin margins, slow scaling | Low |
| Water merchant | Testing and filtering | Liability claims from bad batches | Low–moderate |
| Chemist | Closely guarded recipes | Addiction fallout, raids | Moderate–high |
| Gunsmith | Permits and modifications | Inspections, confiscation | High |
| Fence (stolen goods) | Authentication and appraisal | Raids, mob justice | High |
| Organ dealer | Careful appraisal | Severe legal and faction blowback | Very high |

The pattern is consistent: verification effort scales with margin. A water merchant who skips filtration is not running a clever hustle, just a delayed liability. A fence who resells without appraising is donating credits to a supplier.

### Where Counterfeits Fit

Relabeling counterfeit product as genuine is the lowest-effort contraband play and the one most likely to backfire socially. It works best against the arrogant elite, who pay premium prices and rarely inspect closely — but reputation is a currency in **Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim contraband** trading, and a burned customer talks.

## Hiding, Bribing, or Reporting: Your Three Exits

When Security closes in, the game gives you a small but meaningful decision set. Approve the sale, refuse it, report it, or look away — and someone is always taking notes. The same logic applies to the goods themselves.

| Option | Cost | Best used when | Downside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hide the stock | Inventory space, staging effort | You have a real hiding spot and a straight face | A failed search costs the whole stash |
| Bribe your way out | Upfront credits | You are caught but liquid | Repeated payoffs drain rent reserves |
| Report the supplier | Relationship with that supplier | You need reputation with Security or a faction | Loses a supply line permanently |
| Look away / refuse the sale | Lost profit | The customer reads as a security fisher | No upside beyond safety |

Bribing is the most misunderstood tool in the kit. It is not a get-out-of-jail card; it is a tax on sloppy operations. Players who lean on bribes as a primary strategy usually discover that rent outpaces their payoff budget by mid-run.

## Reading the Counter: Customers Are the Real Detection System

Most contraband losses do not start with a raid. They start with a customer who was never a customer. The game's own framing is blunt: today's buyer could be a security fisher gathering evidence, a revolutionary seeking leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you are still useful.

Your job behind the counter is pattern recognition under time pressure. A few practical habits separate a profitable shopkeeper from a confiscated one:

1. **Watch for repeated visits with no purchase.** Evidence collectors come back.
2. **Price-test with a small sale first.** A low-stakes transaction reveals how a customer reacts to being asked for details.
3. **Track which faction the customer likely serves.** Faction connections unlock exclusive deals, but they also mean your name travels.
4. **Never sell contraband to a first-time buyer at full margin.** The margin is the tell.

## Building a Contraband Economy That Survives Rent

Rent is the clock. It rises, and confiscated stock or one bad deal can leave you short on the next payment. That single constraint should shape every contraband decision you make.

| Phase | Contraband posture | Priority | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | None or minimal | Learn verification on legal goods | Spending your float on a "deal" |
| Stable | Small, high-turnover batches | Build two rent payments of buffer | Over-hiding stock you cannot move |
| Scaling | Layered lines with one clean anchor | Unlock underground licenses | Spreading reputation too thin |
| Expansion | Multiple businesses, contract work | Production chains and machines | Assuming the buffer covers a raid |

Practical rules that hold up across runs:

- **Keep one clean product line.** It funds the rent while the contraband line compounds.
- **Never let contraband exceed what you can lose in one raid.** Confiscation is a wipe of that category, not a fine.
- **Authenticate before you haggle.** Appraisal protects margin; haggling only protects price.
- **Treat reputation as armor and as a target.** Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back.
- **Sell counterfeit to the elite, sell honest goods to the desperate.** Exploiting the less fortunate has a way of returning with interest.

## FAQ

**Is contraband required to win in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim?**
No. Legal lines like water and hydroponics can carry a run, but contraband is the faster path to the buyout price. The keyword in the design is "faster" — not "safer."

**How do I stop raids from wiping my Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim contraband stock?**
Spread risk across categories, keep a hiding spot that is not your main shelf, and maintain enough liquid credits to bribe once without missing rent. One raid should sting, not end the run.

**What is the best first contraband item for a new shopkeeper?**
Fenced stolen goods with a clean authentication check. They teach appraisal and hiding simultaneously with a smaller legal footprint than weapons or chems.

**Can I report a supplier and keep buying from them later?**
Community reports suggest reporting damages the relationship, so treat it as a one-way door. Report when the reputation gain with Security or a faction outweighs the supply line.

**Does selling counterfeit goods hurt my reputation?**
It can. Counterfeits to the elite are the intended low-risk play; counterfeits to regulars tend to surface later, and mob justice is explicitly on the table in this station.
