## The Main Vectors for Customer Theft at the Counter

Not every loss is a shoplifter sprinting for the door. The game's own description warns that "customers push their luck" and that contraband often arrives with problems you only notice after the deal is done. That single line explains most of the theft you will experience.

| Theft Vector | How It Plays Out | Early Tell | Cost If Missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walk-off theft | Customer grabs stock during a crowded rush | Loitering near high-value shelves, no purchase intent | Full retail value of the item |

Treat every one of these as a pricing problem, not a moral one. The question is never "was that fair?" It is "did this transaction clear my rent contribution for the day?"

## Reading the Customer Before the Sale Closes

The store page is explicit that augs — people carrying the rogue sentient AI chip — look like everyone else until they do not. That is the whole detection game in miniature: watch for behavior that does not match the story.

Player experience suggests the fastest read is pacing. Honest customers browse. Thieves manage the clock — they want the sale finished before you look closely at anything.

### A Four-Step Inspection Routine

1. **Inspect visually first.** Cigarette packs and sealed goods need a visual pass before resale, and that same habit catches swapped packaging.
2. **Test anything liquid.** Water must be tested and filtered; untreated stock is a liability claim waiting to happen.
3. **Appraise before you buy.** Rare items need proper appraisal to avoid overpaying, and overpaying is theft with extra steps.
4. **Re-check the counter after the customer leaves.** Swaps happen in the handoff, not on the shelf.

Run those four steps for a full in-game day and you will notice how many losses were actually appraisal failures rather than pickpocketing.

## Countermeasures That Pay for Themselves

The right countermeasure depends on your product line, because each trade brings its own kind of trouble. Weapons bring inspections. Chemicals bring addiction. Water brings liability. Contraband brings raids. The better the margin, the worse the consequences when something slips.

| Countermeasure | Upfront Cost | Theft Protection | Trade-Off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visual inspection routine | Free, costs time | High against swaps and fakes | Slower service during rushes |
| Appraisal before purchase | Small fee or skill investment | High against overpaying | Reduces deal volume |
| Hiding stolen goods | Storage space | Blocks routine inspections | Raid risk if Security searches |

| Refusing suspicious buyers | Lost sale | Eliminates most bad-faith deals | Word spreads; fewer high-margin offers |
| Reporting to Security | Free | Builds standing with authorities | Marks you as an informant to others |

### Layout and Stock Control

Placement is a free theft countermeasure and most players never touch it. Keep your highest-value and most restricted inventory behind the counter or in hidden storage, and keep the cheap, fast-moving goods out front where browsing is expected. If a customer has to ask for an item, you get a second look at them — and a second chance to catch a fake permit.

The same logic applies to expansion. Running multiple businesses multiplies income, but it also multiplies the number of counters you cannot watch at once. Expand only after your first shop runs a full cycle without a mystery shortage.

The game hands you four verbs and no moral scoreboard. The store page promises reputation buys access and protection — and paints a target on your back at the same time. | Choice | Immediate Result | Reputation Effect | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|---|

A practical rule from community discussion: never report a customer whose faction you rely on for exclusive deals. Take the loss, tighten your layout, and keep the supply line open. Conversely, if Security already suspects you, a well-placed report can buy you weeks of quiet.

## FAQ

**How do I stop customer theft in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim?**
Layer three habits: inspect every item visually before and after a sale, appraise rare goods before you buy them, and keep restricted stock in hidden storage rather than on open shelves. Most losses come from appraisal failures and swaps, not from dramatic robberies.

**Can customers sell me stolen goods by accident or on purpose?**
Yes. Suppliers overstate value and contraband arrives with problems you only discover after the deal closes. Treat every inbound offer as a claim to verify, and remember that fencing discounted stolen goods is profitable only if Security never reaches you.

**Should I report a thief or look away?**
Report when you need Security protection or your standing with authorities is shaky. Look away when the buyer belongs to a faction that supplies your best deals. The Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim customer theft choice is a reputation trade, not a moral one.

**Do augs steal more often than other customers?**
Augs are not automatically thieves — they are undetectable until a tell reveals them. The real risk is that misreading a customer's signs carries its own price either way, so build a routine that catches pattern breaks instead of guessing by appearance.
