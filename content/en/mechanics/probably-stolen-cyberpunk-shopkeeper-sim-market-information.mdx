## Pricing Strategy: Markup, Haggle, and Margin

Pricing is where most runs are won or lost. A shop that sells 40 items at a thin margin usually beats a shop that sells six at a fat one — but only if traffic supports it. The trick is to price by category rather than by gut feeling, then adjust daily.

| Pricing strategy | Markup band | Best used for | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss leader | At or below cost | Pulling traffic to a new shop | Eats cash if overused |
| Standard markup | Modest, steady | Everyday consumables | Slow, unexciting profit |
| Premium | High | Rare or quest-relevant items | Sits on the shelf for days |
| Opportunistic | Very high | Shortages and event windows | Reputation damage if it lingers |

Actionable habits that hold up across builds:

- **Reprice every cycle.** A tag that was right on day one is often wrong by day four.
- **Set a margin floor.** Decide the lowest percentage you'll accept and refuse deals below it.
- **Watch sell-through, not just sales.** An item that sells in one cycle is underpriced; one that never sells is overpriced.
- **Discount selectively.** Clear dead categories, never the items buyers are already fighting over.
- **Keep one premium anchor.** A single expensive, desirable item raises the perceived value of everything near it.

Haggling deserves its own note. Accepting the first counteroffer teaches the game that you'll always fold. Hold firm on high-demand goods and concede on slow movers — that combination protects both margin and reputation.

The economics are straightforward: the deeper the discount, the more of your margin is really payment for risk. A lot that costs a fraction of retail is only a bargain if the goods move before anyone asks where they came from. If your storefront already draws attention, pay the premium for clean stock and sleep better.

A useful balance is roughly 70% safe sourcing, 20% speculative, and 10% genuinely risky — enough upside to grow, not enough exposure to end a run in one bad night.

## Common Market Mistakes to Avoid

- **Buying because it's cheap.** A discount on something nobody wants is a loss with extra steps.
- **Pricing everything at maximum.** Traffic dies quietly, and quiet traffic is hard to notice until rent is due.
- **Ignoring the cost of shelf space.** Every slot holding a dud is a slot not earning.
- **Over-investing in risky stock early.** Build a cash cushion before you chase the high-margin lots.
- **Never upgrading.** Storage and counter upgrades compound; a bigger, faster shop multiplies every good decision you already make.
- **Treating one good night as a trend.** One spike is noise. Three cycles is a pattern.
