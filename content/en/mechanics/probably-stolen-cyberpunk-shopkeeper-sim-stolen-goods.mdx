## Read the Seller Before You Read the Item

Every Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim stolen goods deal starts with a person, not an object. The customer across your counter could be a Security fisher collecting evidence, a revolutionary hunting leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you are still useful. Suppliers inflate value, buyers push their luck, and contraband often carries problems you only discover after the credits change hands.

**A practical seller check, in order:**

1. **Ask where it came from.** A vague or rehearsed story is a pricing signal, not a disqualifier.
2. **Inspect markings and serials.** Anything that can be traced back to a registered owner is a future confiscation.
3. **Weigh and feel the product.** Cut quality and filler are common, and they cost you the difference at resale.
4. **Check whether it is legal to sell at all.** Weapons need permits; chemicals invite scrutiny.
5. **Watch who else is in the room.** A nervous seller with a patient friend outside is a setup.

## Appraisal, Counterfeits, and the Quality-Cut Temptation

Rare goods are where careless shopkeepers bleed. Appraisal exists so you do not overpay for something a supplier swears is genuine, and skipping it is the same as donating credits. Counterfeiting is the mirror image: relabeling junk as premium product is one of the fastest margins in Probably Stolen, right up until a buyer with connections notices.

| Appraisal check | Why it matters | Cost of skipping it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provenance story | Inconsistent history flags a trap | You buy a tracked item |
| Serial and markings | Ties goods to a registry | Confiscation on inspection |
| Weight and finish | Reveals cut or diluted product | You pay premium for filler |
| Recipe or permit | Confirms it is legal to sell | Fines and repeat inspections |
| Buyer profile | Identifies undercover checks | A raid you invited |

Player experience from the launch window points to the same lesson repeatedly: the margin you protect by skipping appraisal is smaller than the loss from a single bad purchase. Appraise the expensive stuff, every time. Cheap goods are a rounding error; a fake artifact is a rent payment.

Hiding stolen merchandise from the authorities is an explicit part of the fantasy, and bribery is the fallback when hiding fails. The mistake most new shopkeepers make is stacking heat in one place — a back room full of identical unregistered weapons reads as a business model, not a hobby.

| Threat | What triggers it | Warning sign | Counter-move |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Security sweep | High concentration of contraband | The same "customer" returning often | Spread stock, keep clean inventory in front |
| Faction friction | Overt favoritism toward one group | Suppliers going cold | Rotate deals across factions |
| Mob justice | Selling bad or dangerous product locally | Angry crowds gathering | Refund fast, tighten quality control |

Community reports describe heat as cumulative rather than random — the game remembers how many risky sales you have approved, refused, reported, or ignored. That means a quiet week is a legitimate strategy. Sit on the merchandise, sell boring legal stock, and let attention drift elsewhere before you move the good stuff.

## FAQ

**What counts as stolen goods in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim?**

Anything of questionable origin that you acquire from a supplier and resell — unregistered weapons, untraceable chemicals, mislabeled cigarettes, and discounted contraband. The game flags these items through their provenance and inspection results rather than a single label, so appraisal is how you identify them.

**Can I get caught selling Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim stolen goods?**

Yes. Security sweeps, faction retaliation, and mob justice are all live consequences. You can hide stock, keep clean inventory in front, or bribe your way out of a bust, but repeated risky sales accumulate attention over time.

**Should I relabel counterfeit products or just fence them cheaply?**

Relabeling pays more but damages reputation and invites retaliation from buyers with power. Cheap fencing is safer and faster. Early on, speed usually beats margin because rent does not wait.

**How do I balance stolen merchandise with a legal product line?**

Use legal goods to cover fixed costs and stolen goods to build the buyout fund. Keep the risky stock small, appraised, and hidden, and never let one seizure wipe out the cash you need for the next payment.
