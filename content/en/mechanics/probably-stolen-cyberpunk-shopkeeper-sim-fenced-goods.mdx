Fencing is both a starting profession and a universal mechanic. Even a hydroponic farmer can quietly move a crate of stolen cigarettes on the side. What changes is your access, your cover story, and how much heat you can absorb before the station turns on you.

Three things make an item "fenced" rather than ordinary stock:

- **Origin you can't document** — no supplier chain, no permit, no receipt.
- **A discount that smells wrong** — desperate sellers dump inventory far below market rate.
- **A story you have to sell** — you relabel, reframe, or simply refuse to explain.

## The Five-Step Fencing Loop

Every profitable transaction in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim involving fenced goods follows the same rhythm: acquire cheap, verify hard, hide well, sell to the right face, and survive the fallout. Skip a step and the margin you gained gets eaten by a fine, a confiscation, or a very personal visit.

| Step | What You Do | What Goes Wrong If You Skip It |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Source | Buy from desperate locals, faction brokers, or your own production chains | You overpay because a supplier overstated value |
| 2. Appraise | Inspect rare items, test consumables, check weapons for modifications | You absorb the loss on a counterfeit or a dud |
| 3. Sanitize | Relabel packaging, adjust quality claims, strip identifying marks | A routine inspection flags your shelf instantly |
| 4. Hide | Stash contraband away from inspection zones and keep clean stock visible | One sweep wipes a full night of profit |
| 5. Sell | Match the item to the buyer who wants it and won't report it | You move volume to the wrong customer and gain heat |

Appraisal is where most new shopkeepers bleed creds. The game explicitly warns that suppliers inflate value and that contraband often arrives with problems you only discover after the deal closes. Build the habit of inspecting before you pay, not after. A rare item you can't verify is a lottery ticket, not an asset.

Haggling is the second skill to drill. Every bargain comes with a trade-off — push too hard and you lose the seller, push too little and your margin vanishes. Treat haggling as a margin tool for legitimate stock and a heat tool for fenced stock: sometimes paying the asking price is worth it to keep a supplier quiet.

## Product Lines, Risk, and the Margins Behind Them

Each product line in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim carries its own failure mode, and fenced goods amplify whatever risk you already accepted. Weapons bring inspections. Chemicals bring addiction and recipe theft. Water brings liability. Contraband brings raids. Choosing your inventory is really choosing which kind of trouble you're willing to monetize.

| Product Line | Trouble Trigger | Fencing Upside | Watch Out For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weapons | Permit inspections and serial checks | High margin on modified pieces | Every sale is traceable |
| Chemicals | Addiction, guarded recipes | Repeat buyers, strong volume | Recipe leaks and liability |
| Water | Testing and filtration costs | Steady, low-drama income | Thin margins, high upkeep |
| Cigarettes & consumables | Visual inspection before resale | Fast turnover, easy stashing | Counterfeit labeling |
| Rare artifacts | Appraisal errors | Biggest single-ticket sales | Overpaying on a bad read |
| Stolen lots | Raids and mob justice | Cheapest acquisition cost | Heat spikes fast |

Two shortcuts sit on top of that table: cutting product quality and relabeling counterfeits as genuine. Both raise your margin and both damage the thing that actually protects you — reputation. Selling fake luxury goods to the arrogant elite is one of the game's most satisfying plays, but it's also the fastest way to make an enemy with resources.

A practical rule: never let fenced stock exceed roughly the portion of your inventory you can afford to lose in a single raid. Confiscation doesn't just cost you the goods; it can leave you short on the next rent payment.

## Heat Management: Hiding, Bribing, and Surviving a Raid

Hiding stolen goods and contraband from the authorities is a core feature, not a flavor detail. If you get caught, bribing your way out is often cheaper than losing the stock — but it's a recurring cost that eats the margin you fought for.

| Tactic | Effect | What It Costs You |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stash contraband off-shelf | Delays discovery during sweeps | Wasted shelf space and slower sales |
| Bribe an officer | Clears immediate trouble | Creds, and it may not be a one-time fee |
| Report the seller | Shifts blame away from you | Underworld reputation |
| Refuse the deal | Zero added heat | Zero profit |
| Blend with legal stock | Makes fencing harder to spot | Requires a functioning cover business |

Then there's mob justice. Security is a system you can bribe or outmaneuver; a crowd that decides you sold them poisoned water or a counterfeit aug is not. Community reports suggest that the worst outcomes usually come from stacking too many risks on the same customer base — bad product sold cheap to the same people who know where you live.

The safest pattern is separation: keep your legitimate customers buying legitimate goods, and route fenced stock to buyers who benefit from silence.

## FAQ

**Can you fence goods with any starting profession?**
Yes. Fencing is available to every build, but the Fence start gives you the cheapest access to stolen lots and the fewest questions asked. Other professions trade a slower ramp for better cover.

**What happens if Security catches you with stolen stock?**
Expect confiscation and a financial hit. Bribing your way out is often the cheaper option, especially if the lot was expensive. The real damage is usually the ripple effect on rent and expansion plans.

**Is selling counterfeit goods to elites worth the risk?**
It's one of the highest-margin plays in the game and one of the most dangerous. Elites have resources and long memories, so treat counterfeit sales as a limited tool rather than a business model.

**How do you avoid mob justice when moving fenced goods?**
Keep quality cuts and counterfeit labels away from your regulars, avoid stacking multiple risk factors on one customer group, and never let contraband dominate your shelves. Reputation is the shield that keeps the crowd from turning on you.
