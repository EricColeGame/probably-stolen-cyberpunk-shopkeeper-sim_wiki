## How the Appraisal System Fits Into a Shop Day

Appraisal in Probably Stolen is not an optional minigame you can click past. It is the gate that sits in front of every purchase, every haggle, and every resale. The game's own description is blunt about it: suppliers overstate value, customers push their luck, and contraband often arrives with problems you only discover after the deal is closed.

That means your margins are not really set by your price tags. They are set by how accurately you judge what is sitting on the counter in front of you. Misread the item, or misread the person selling it, and you absorb the loss yourself.

| Appraisal mistake | Immediate cost | Downstream damage |
|---|---|---|
| Overpaying a supplier for "rare" stock | Cash margin evaporates | Rent payment comes up short |
| Missing a hidden defect in contraband | Resale value collapses | Customer trust drops |
| Accepting a counterfeit as genuine | You paid full price for a fake | Backlash when you resell it |
| Underselling your own inventory | Lost profit per unit | Slower expansion and licenses |
| Skipping permit checks on weapons | Stock confiscated | Inspection heat on your shop |

The pattern is consistent: a bad appraisal rarely kills you on the spot. It quietly removes the buffer you needed for the next rent cycle.

## Reading the Seller Before You Read the Item

Community reports consistently point to the same habit among players who stay profitable: they appraise the person first. The counter is described in-game as a parade of unknowns — a security fisher hunting for evidence, a revolutionary looking for leverage, a fallen elite testing whether you are useful.

Each of those archetypes handles money differently, and each one is a different appraisal problem. A nervous seller dumping goods cheap is not automatically a bargain; it may be stolen stock with a tracking problem attached. A smooth talker with a perfect story is often the one padding a valuation.

| Seller type | Typical tell | Smart move |
|---|---|---|
| Desperate hauler | Rushes the sale, avoids eye contact | Slow down, inspect fully, negotiate hard |
| Smooth talker | Detailed backstory, vague paperwork | Verify origin before agreeing to a number |
| Fallen elite | Condescending, wants a fast exit | Lowball politely; they rarely comparison-shop |
| Security fisher | Asks about your stock, not your prices | Keep contraband out of sight; stay neutral |
| Fellow fence | Knows your margins better than you do | Trade item-for-item instead of paying cash |

Player experience suggests that the single cheapest upgrade to your appraisal accuracy is simply refusing to buy on the first offer. Almost every seller in the game has room to move, and the ones who do not are usually the ones worth walking away from.

## A Practical Item Appraisal Checklist

Not every product line fails the same way, and the appraisal system reflects that. Cigarette packs need a visual inspection before resale. Water has to be tested and filtered. Weapons require permits and the right modifications. Profitable chemicals depend on closely guarded recipes. Rare goods need a genuine appraisal, or you will overpay for something you cannot move.

| Item category | Required appraisal step | Red flag to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarettes and consumables | Visual inspection of packaging | Seals that look resealed or reprinted |
| Water | Test and filter before selling | Cloudiness, unusual container weight |
| Weapons | Permit check plus modification review | Missing paperwork, mismatched serials |
| Chemicals | Recipe and purity verification | Supplier who will not name the source |
| Rare artifacts and components | Full appraisal before purchase | Price far below the expected curve |
| Stolen goods | Origin and heat assessment | Seller who insists on a fast, quiet sale |

The "An Eye for Value" achievement is a good mental marker here: the game wants you to build a repeatable habit of looking closer, not to memorize a fixed price list. Prices drift with supply and demand, but the inspection steps do not.

## Turning a Bad Appraisal Into a Deliberate Profit Strategy

Here is where the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim appraisal system gets morally interesting. Once you can spot a flaw, you can also choose to exploit it. The game explicitly supports cutting a product's quality, relabeling counterfeits as genuine, and fencing discounted stolen goods if you believe Security will not reach you.

None of these are free money. They are trades: you swap short-term margin for long-term exposure. The elite pay well for counterfeit goods and rarely inspect closely — until they do. Stolen stock moves fast — until a raid or a bribe wipes out the gain.

| Tactic | Upside | Risk you are accepting |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting product quality | Higher margin per unit | Reputation damage if regulars notice |
| Relabeling counterfeits as genuine | Premium pricing to arrogant elites | Faction retaliation when discovered |
| Fencing stolen goods | Cheap inventory, fast turnover | Security raids and confiscation |
| Bribing your way out | Avoids immediate consequences | Recurring cash drain and dependency |

The honest read: use these tools to survive a bad rent week, not as your permanent business model. Community reports suggest that players who lean fully into counterfeits eventually spend more on damage control than they ever earned on the markup.

## Appraisal, Faction Heat, and the Aug Problem

Appraisal does not happen in a vacuum. Reputation buys you access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back. Every sale is a gamble: approve it, refuse it, report it, or look away, and someone is always taking notes.

There is also a station-wide policy you are expected to enforce. A sentient AI chip went rogue and took its hosts with it, and augmented citizens look like everyone else until they do not. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you are the one holding the gun. Missing the signs, or misreading them, carries its own price — which makes body language and behavioral appraisal just as valuable as item appraisal.

| Heat source | How it builds | How to manage it |
|---|---|---|
| Weapons without permits | Inspections triggered by stock audits | Keep licenses current before scaling |
| Contraband in the open | Raid risk during peak hours | Build hiding spots early |
| Counterfeits sold to elites | Slow-burn faction resentment | Rotate buyers, keep quality believable |
| Missed aug signs | Policy enforcement penalties | Slow down counter interactions |

## A Repeatable Appraisal Workflow

If you want one routine that covers most situations, use this sequence every time a seller walks in.

1. **Pause before pricing.** Let the seller talk first; the opening offer tells you how badly they need the sale.
2. **Identify the category.** Weapons, water, chemicals, consumables, and rare goods each demand a different inspection.
3. **Run the required check.** Visual inspection, testing and filtering, permit review, or full appraisal — never skip the step that matches the item.
4. **Look for the hidden issue.** Assume contraband has a problem you have not spotted yet, and price as if you will find it later.
5. **Set a walk-away number before you negotiate.** Haggling is fine, but every bargain comes with a trade-off.
6. **Log the outcome.** Track what you paid, what you sold it for, and what surprised you — that record becomes your real appraisal skill.

Do this consistently and the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim appraisal system becomes predictable, which is exactly what you want in a game where rent keeps climbing and confiscated stock can end a run.

## FAQ

**Is the appraisal system in Probably Stolen random?**
Not entirely. Item categories have defined inspection steps — testing water, checking weapon permits, appraising rare goods — and those steps reduce your risk. Randomness lives mostly in what sellers hide from you, which is why inspecting carefully and pricing defensively matters.

**What should I appraise first as a new shopkeeper?**
Start with a product line you can fully inspect. Water and consumables are forgiving; weapons and chemicals carry inspection and addiction consequences. Learning the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim appraisal system on low-risk goods keeps early rent payments safe.

**Can I profit from a deliberately bad appraisal?**
Yes, but as a calculated risk. Cutting quality, relabeling counterfeits, and fencing stolen goods all raise margins and all raise heat. Treat them as emergency cash, not a permanent strategy.

**Does appraisal affect factions and reputation?**
Indirectly but heavily. Overpriced junk damages customer trust, counterfeits sold to elites create enemies, and missed aug signs break station policy. In Probably Stolen, accurate appraisal is reputation management wearing a price tag.
