## Pick Your Starting Trade: The First Tool in Your Box

Your opening choice shapes everything that follows, because each trade comes with its own profit curve and its own flavor of disaster. Weapons invite inspections. Chemicals invite addiction. Water invites liability. Contraband invites raids. As the store page puts it, you are not picking a theme for your shop — you are picking which kind of trouble you are willing to monetize.

| Starting path | Where the money is | What can ruin you | Good fit if you like |
|---|---|---|---|

## The Item Toolbox: Inspect, Authenticate, and Appraise

Every item you stock carries its own handling rules, and skipping them is how margins evaporate. Cigarette packs need a visual once-over before resale. Water has to be tested and filtered. Weapons need permits and modifications. Profitable chemicals depend on recipes you have to protect. Rare goods need proper appraisal, or you will overpay for something that looks like a payday and is not.

| Item type | Required action | What happens if you skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection before resale | Counterfeit or damaged stock sold at a loss |
| Water | Test and filter | Liability and unhappy buyers |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | Inspection failures and confiscation |
| Chemicals | Follow closely guarded recipes | Weak batches, addiction fallout |
| Rare goods | Appraisal before purchase | Overpaying on inflated supplier claims |

Beyond inspection, the game gives you three sharper blades: cutting a product's quality, relabeling counterfeit goods as genuine, and fencing discounted stolen merchandise if you believe Security will not reach you. Each one raises your ceiling and lowers your floor at the same time. Suppliers overstate value, customers push their luck, and contraband often hides problems you only discover after the deal closes.

The practical takeaway from this slice of the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim toolbox is simple: your margin is a function of how well you assess what is in front of you. Misjudge the item or the person selling it, and you absorb the cost personally.

## Crafting and Production Tools: Machines, Recipes, and Licenses

Once you can cover rent, the game wants you to stop reselling and start producing. Machines and production chains let you craft valuable artifacts from raw inputs, which is where the real margins live — and where your capital gets tied up if you overbuild.

Think of the production side as a second layer of the toolbox:

- **Machines** convert inputs into finished goods, so a reliable input line matters more than a single big score.
- **Production chains** reward planning; a broken link stalls the whole line while rent keeps ticking.
- **Advanced licenses** unlock access to the underground market, opening inventory you cannot buy openly.
- **Additional jobs** — water merchant, hydroponic farmer, organ dealer, and more — stack extra income streams on top of your storefront.
- **Expansion** lets you run multiple businesses and unlock new merchandise, but only after the current one earns its keep.

A useful rule from player experience: never buy a machine you cannot feed. A chain that runs at 60% capacity still costs you full rent, and confiscated stock or one bad deal can leave you short on the next payment. Build the input line first, then the machine, then the upgrade.

## Risk Management: Factions, Security, and the Rent Clock

You never really know who is on the other side of the counter. Today's customer might be a Security fisher collecting evidence, a revolutionary hunting leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you are still useful. Every sale is a decision: approve it, refuse it, report it, or look away. Someone is always taking notes.

| Situation | Your options | The trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Customer looks like a Security informant | Serve, refuse, or report them | Refusing costs a sale; serving costs exposure |
| Contraband in stock during a sweep | Hide it or bribe your way out | Hiding risks confiscation; bribes drain cash |
| A faction wants an exclusive deal | Commit, stall, or play both sides | Loyalty buys protection but paints a target |
| A customer may be an Aug | Use the enforcement tool you are issued, or misread the signs | Missing the signs, or misreading them, carries its own price |
| A supplier inflates a valuation | Haggle or walk away | Haggling wins margin; pushing too hard kills the deal |

Reputation is the most valuable non-item tool in the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim toolbox. It buys access and protection, but it also puts a target on your back, so spend it deliberately. The station-wide Aug policy — a rogue sentient AI chip that took its hosts with it — is a good example: every faction agrees on the rule, and you are the one holding the tool to enforce it, which means a wrong call is entirely yours.

Then there is the clock. Rent rises, confiscated stock hurts, and the end goal is buying out the shop entirely. Every purchase needs to earn its keep, and every expansion should shorten the distance to that buyout rather than lengthen it. If you are unsure whether to spend, ask whether the item pays for itself before the next rent cycle. If the answer is no, it is not a tool — it is a trophy.
