## Appraisal: Where Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Item Prices Are Really Decided

Most players negotiate first and inspect second, which is backwards. Appraisal is the step that converts an unknown object into a priced asset, and skipping it means you are quoting blind on someone else's terms.

| Appraisal action | What it reveals | Price impact |
|---|---|---|
| Visual inspection | Obvious damage, tampering, mismatched parts | Prevents overpaying on junk |

| Authentication | True origin and legitimacy of rare goods | Unlocks collector-tier quotes |

A useful habit: appraise anything you did not personally craft or buy from a trusted supplier. ## A Repeatable Pricing Workflow for Every Sale

Random haggling produces random results. Use the same sequence every time and your margins become predictable, which matters enormously once rent starts climbing.

1. **Establish your true cost.** Include purchase price, repair or modification spend, and the days the item sat on your shelf.
2. **Appraise before you talk money.** Know the item's condition and authenticity tier.
3. **Read the customer.** Faction patches, visible augs, and urgency tell you how much patience they have.
4. **Open high but plausible.** An absurd first number kills the conversation; a firm one anchors it.
5. **Trade concessions, not discounts.** Throw in a cheap accessory or faster delivery instead of shaving the price.
6. **Log the result.** Note what sold, to whom, and at what markup so your next quote is informed.

| Margin band | When to use it | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Thin and fast | Basics, perishables, rent week | Low profit, high liquidity |
| Standard | Everyday weapons, chems, components | Balanced, safe |
| Fat and slow | Authenticated rares, contraband lots | Big paydays, big exposure |

Player experience suggests the fat band is where fortunes are made — but only if you can survive the wait without missing rent.

## Faction Standing, Rent, and the Real Cost of Holding Inventory

Faction politics are not flavor text; they are a pricing multiplier. A customer who trusts you will accept a firmer quote, refer others, and warn you before an inspection. A customer who despises you will haggle every transaction to the bone and report you the moment it is convenient.

| Faction buyer | What they pay for | Pricing caution |
|---|---|---|
| Station security | Reliability, clean paperwork | Never sell them visibly stolen goods |
| Revolutionaries | Volume, deniability | High risk if you are seen dealing |
| Fallen elites | Authenticity and discretion | They expect premium service |
| Criminals and smugglers | Contraband, no questions | Highest margin, highest heat |
| Workers and traders | Fair basics, repeat business | Thin margins, loyal volume |

Holding inventory is never free. Every day an item sits unsold, it competes with the rent you owe and the stock you could have rotated instead. If a lot has not moved by the time the next payment looms, discount it — a small loss beats an eviction.

## FAQ

**How do I know if I'm overcharging for an item?**
Watch the customer's reaction and their walk-away point. If buyers consistently accept your first quote without haggling, your baseline is too low. If they leave immediately, you opened too high. Adjust in small steps rather than swinging wildly.

**Do Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim item prices change over time?**
Yes. Rent increases, faction politics, station events, and shortages all shift what buyers will pay. A price sheet built early in a run should be revised regularly, especially after any major faction development.

**Should I specialize or sell everything?**
Specialize first. Gunsmithing, chemistry, and fencing each have distinct appraisal needs and risk profiles, and splitting capital across all three early leaves you mediocre at everything. Expand once one income stream is stable.

**Is contraband worth the risk to my margins?**
It carries the highest ceilings in the game, but inspections and faction attention can wipe an entire inventory. Sell contraband in small lots, keep it separated from clean stock, and never let it become the majority of what you are holding.

**What is the fastest way to improve Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim item prices early?**
Appraise everything, build one strong faction relationship, and rotate stock aggressively instead of holding for a perfect buyer. Liquidity beats perfection when rent is due.
