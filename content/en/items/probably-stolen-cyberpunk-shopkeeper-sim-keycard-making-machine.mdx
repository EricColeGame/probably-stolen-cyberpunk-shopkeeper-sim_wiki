## What the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Keycard Making Machine Actually Does

Questing Goose Studio built the shop loop around production chains: you manage machines, feed them materials, and craft artifacts worth far more than their parts. The keycard maker sits squarely in that system. It is a conversion machine — components in, finished access hardware out — and it rewards players who treat it like a small factory rather than a vending slot.

In practice, the machine converts a handful of low-value inputs into a finished card that sells at a multiple of its component cost. Player experience suggests the bottleneck is rarely the machine itself; it is sourcing consistent components and finding buyers who will not report you.

- **Inputs to watch for:** blank card stock, chip or circuitry components, and casing or housing materials.
- **Outputs:** access cards of varying clearance levels, from basic worker passes to restricted hardware.
- **Gating:** expect to need a license or permit tier before the higher-clearance recipes open up.
- **Footprint:** production machines eat floor space and power, so plan your back room before you buy.

Because the machine runs in the background while you haggle at the counter, it is one of the few income streams that scales without you standing still. That makes it ideal for players who want to expand into multiple businesses later.

## Setting Up a Keycard Line: Step-by-Step

Do not buy the machine first and figure out the supply chain later. The Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim keycard making machine only earns when it is fed, and component droughts are the number one reason new shopkeepers abandon the line. Work through this checklist in order.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Unlock the relevant production license | Higher-clearance recipes and underground buyers stay locked otherwise |
| 2 | Place the machine away from the counter | Keeps casual inspections away from your production floor |
| 3 | Lock in a component supplier | A steady flow of blanks and chips protects your margins |

Second, buyer screening — the game's own design notes make clear that every customer could be a security fisher, a revolutionary, or a fallen elite testing your usefulness. Your keycard line concentrates that risk, because access hardware is exactly what all three groups want.

## Keycard Tiers and Where Each One Sells

Tier names shift between builds, but the underlying structure holds. The Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim keycard making machine outputs hardware along a clearance ladder, and each rung attracts a different crowd. Selling above your reputation level is how shops get raided.

| Card Tier | Typical Buyers | Detection Risk | Best Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic access | Workers and low-tier customers | Low | Volume sales and reputation building |
| Staff or utility | Station employees, faction go-betweens | Medium | Steady mid-game income |
| Restricted | Smugglers, revolutionaries | High | Short, high-margin bursts |
| Relabeled "premium" | Arrogant elites | High | Counterfeit profit with real consequences |

The smart play is a layered shelf: basic cards for the counter to keep foot traffic and reputation healthy, restricted cards hidden in back for buyers you trust. That way a surprise inspection finds a boring, legitimate shop, and your real margin lives somewhere else.

## Profit, Risk, and the Paper Trail

Machines make you money; documentation gets you caught. The game repeatedly reminds you that confiscated stock or a single bad deal can leave you short on rent, and a keycard operation generates exactly the kind of paper trail Security loves.

| Risk | What Triggers It | How to Mitigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspection | Selling restricted tiers openly | Move stock to hidden storage, keep display shelves clean |
| Raid | High contraband volume or faction heat | Spread inventory, avoid stacking one high-risk item |
| Bad components | Cheap, overstated supplier goods | Test a batch before committing to bulk |
| Reputation hit | Counterfeit cards sold to the wrong buyer | Match product to customer, not to your greed |
| Rent squeeze | Over-investing in machines too early | Keep one rent cycle in reserve |

Bribes are a legitimate tool, not a failure state. If you get caught, paying your way out is often cheaper than losing the inventory and the reputation. Budget for it the same way you budget for components.

### Haggle like you mean it

Every bargain in this game comes with a trade-off, and keycards are no exception. Pushing too hard on price can flag you as a problem seller; accepting the first offer bleeds margin. Aim for the middle: a fair price, a clean transaction, and a customer who leaves without a grudge.

## Five Mistakes That Wreck a Keycard Run

1. **Buying the machine before securing components.** Idle machines still cost you rent.
2. **Selling restricted cards at the open counter.** You are advertising your own inspection.
3. **Skipping appraisal on supplier stock.** Overpaying once can erase three good sales.
4. **Ignoring faction relationships.** Exclusive deals and protection come from connections, not from volume.
5. **Reinvesting every credit.** Rising rent does not care about your expansion plans.

If something feels off, check the symptom against this quick table.

## FAQ

**Is the keycard making machine worth buying early?**
Yes, if you already have a component supplier lined up. It is cheaper to start than a weapons bench and scales better than water sales. The Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim keycard making machine becomes a genuine profit engine once you can feed it consistently.

**Can I sell restricted keycards without getting caught?**
Sometimes, but not reliably at the open counter. Use hidden storage, sell to buyers you have vetted, and keep a bribe budget ready. Community reports suggest repeated high-tier sales draw Security attention fast.

**Does the machine replace my other product lines?**
No — it complements them. Keycards give you steady mid-tier margin, but licenses, faction deals, and rare-item appraisal still drive the biggest paydays. Treat the machine as one leg of a diversified shop.

**What should I do first if I keep failing inspections?**
Clear restricted stock off your display shelves, sell basic cards for a few cycles to rebuild reputation, then reintroduce higher tiers only to trusted buyers.
