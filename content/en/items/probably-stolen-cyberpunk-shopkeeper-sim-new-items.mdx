## Authentication: The Step That Makes or Breaks New Items

Every item you stock carries its own inspection ritual. Skip it and you absorb the cost of someone else's dishonesty — suppliers overstate value, customers push their luck, and contraband frequently arrives with problems you only notice after the deal closes.

| Item type | Required check | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection before resale | Counterfeit complaints |
| Water | Testing and filtration | Liability claims |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | Inspections, confiscation |
| Chemicals | Closely guarded recipes | Quality collapse, addiction fallout |
| Rare goods | Proper appraisal | Overpaying on the buy-in |
| Contraband | Concealment from Security | Raids, stock seizure |

Three profitable shortcuts sit on top of this system: cutting a product's quality, relabeling counterfeit goods as genuine, and fencing discounted stolen merchandise if you believe Security won't reach you. All three widen your margins immediately. All three invite mob justice if the wrong customer notices.

The appraisal step is where most new players bleed credits. Rare items look identical to mediocre ones until you inspect them, and overpaying once can erase the profit from three good sales. Treat appraisal as a non-negotiable cost of doing business rather than a step you can skip when you're in a hurry.

## FAQ

**Where do Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim new items come from?**
They unlock through progression rather than drops: paying rent to expand, buying advanced licenses for the underground market, building machines and production chains, and earning faction connections that open exclusive deals.

**Which starting profession gives the best item selection?**
The fence and the gunsmith see high-value goods earliest, but both carry heavy risk — raids and inspections respectively. The chemist offers strong margins with recipe and addiction exposure, while water and farming are steadier but slower. Pick based on which consequence you can manage, not which item sounds coolest.

**Can I sell counterfeit or stolen goods safely?**
You can cut quality, relabel counterfeits as genuine, and fence stolen stock — but each shortcut carries a discovery risk. Player experience suggests keeping counterfeit volume low and mixing it into otherwise clean inventory draws less attention than a shelf full of suspicious bargains.

**How do I avoid losing everything to a raid?**
Keep a rent buffer, hide contraband before expanding, and maintain faction relationships that give you cover or an out. If Security does catch you, bribery is usually cheaper than losing the stock — budget for it the same way you budget for restocking.
