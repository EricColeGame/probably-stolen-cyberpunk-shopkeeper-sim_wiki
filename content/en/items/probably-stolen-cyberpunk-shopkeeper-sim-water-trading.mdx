## How Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Water Trading Actually Works

Water handling is a four-step pipeline, and skipping any step is how new merchants bleed money. The game explicitly requires water to be tested and filtered before it is safe to resell, and both steps consume time and resources that you must factor into your asking price.

1. **Acquire stock.** Buy from suppliers, draw from your own hydroponic setup, or take a discount on goods of questionable origin.
2. **Test.** Confirm the batch is what the seller claims. Untested water is a liability ticking clock.
3. **Filter.** Clean the product to a sellable standard. Filtering is where quality — and your reputation — is decided.
4. **Price and sell.** Match the batch to the right customer. A wealthy elite and a desperate dock worker are not the same sale.

| Handling step | What it protects | What it costs you | Skip it and… |
|---|---|---|---|
| Testing | Reputation, health liability | Time and consumables | You resell a bad batch and eat the fallout |
| Filtering | Product quality grade | Machine time, upkeep | Quality drops, margins collapse |
| Relabeling | Short-term margin | Long-term trust | Counterfeit reputation sticks to you |
| Customer matching | Sale price | Patience | You undersell to the wrong buyer |

Community reports suggest that the biggest beginner mistake is treating water like a commodity with one price. It isn't. Every batch carries a different risk profile, and your job is to price that risk correctly.

## Supplier Vetting: Reading the Deal Before You Pay

Suppliers in Probably Stolen overstate value. Customers push their luck. Contraband often arrives with problems you only notice after the deal is closed. Your margin depends entirely on how well you assess what — and who — is in front of you.

| Red flag | What it usually means | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| Price far below market | Stolen, spoiled, or mislabeled | Test before paying, or walk away |
| Sealed containers, no inspection | Seller knows it fails testing | Demand a sample |
| Vague origin story | Security may be tracking it | Reduce volume or refuse |
| Pressure to close fast | Classic overstatement tactic | Slow down; the deal will wait |
| Seller overstates quality | You absorb the cost later | Appraise, don't assume |

For water specifically, the cheapest legal habit is refusing to buy anything you cannot test on the spot. A small loss on a rejected lot beats a large liability claim plus a reputation hit.

The practical approach to Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim water trading is to keep a clean legal baseline and treat questionable goods as a bonus, never a foundation. Legal water pays less per unit, but it pays every single day.

## FAQ

**Is water trading really the best way to make money early in Probably Stolen?**
It is the safest, not the richest. Water's low capital requirement and predictable handling make it ideal for learning testing, filtering, and rent math. Players who want bigger margins should move to chemicals or rare goods once they have a cash reserve — but they should learn the fundamentals on water first.

**Do I have to test and filter every water shipment?**
Yes, if you want to keep your reputation intact. Untested water is a liability you are choosing to accept, and a single bad batch can cost more than the entire discount you got on the purchase.

**Can I sell stolen or counterfeit water for a bigger profit?**
You can cut quality or relabel product, and the game supports fencing discounted stolen goods. But the trade-off is real: security attention, mob justice, and a reputation that follows you. Treat it as an occasional play, not a business model.

Contraband pays more per transaction and carries far more risk — raids, confiscation, and bribery. Water pays steadily with manageable risk. Most successful shopkeepers run water as their stable base and use contraband as a margin booster when the heat is low.
