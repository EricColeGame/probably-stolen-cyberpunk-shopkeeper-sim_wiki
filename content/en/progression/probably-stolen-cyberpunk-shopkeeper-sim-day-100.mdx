## How Progression Actually Works Between Day 1 and Day 100

Day 100 is where a shopkeeping run stops being a scrappy kiosk story and becomes an actual business. Reaching Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim day 100 means you've already survived multiple rent hikes, a handful of inspections, and at least one customer who was definitely not who they claimed to be. The number climbs on a schedule, whether your week was brilliant or catastrophic. Fall behind and you don't get a cutscene — you get arithmetic. Store attractiveness drops, fewer customers walk in, revenue slides, and the gap between what you owe and what you're holding gets wider every cycle. ## The Day 100 Checklist: What a Healthy Shop Should Look Like

By the time you're approaching Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim day 100, your shop should look almost boring from the outside. Boring means predictable: steady foot traffic, two or three things you can sell at a reliable margin, and enough cash on hand to absorb one confiscated shipment without missing rent. If any single deal can bankrupt you, you're not ready.

Use this checklist as a gut check around day 70, then again at day 90.

| System | Healthy at day 100 | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Product lines | Two or three complementary lines | Everything riding on one volatile item |
| Licenses | Advanced or underground access unlocked | Still buying purely at retail |
| Production | At least one working machine chain | Pure resale, no crafting |
| Faction standing | One genuine ally, no active enemies | Everyone neutral or openly hostile |
| Security heat | Manageable, with bribes budgeted | Inspections arriving back to back |
| Cash reserve | Covers several rent cycles | Living week to week |
| Inventory | Appraised, sorted, and hidden properly | Unlabeled stock piling up |

Three non-negotiables stand out:

- **A bribe fund you never touch.** Treat it as an operating cost, not a rainy-day luxury. Getting caught with contraband and having no cash to smooth it over is the most common way a good run ends.
- **A second revenue stream before day 50.** Single-line shops are fragile because a raid, a failed inspection, or a supplier drying up removes your entire income at once.
- **A hiding spot that scales.** Early hiding places are small. If your contraband volume grows but your concealment doesn't, Security eventually finds the overflow.

## Product Lines Ranked by Risk and Return

The single biggest mistake players make heading into Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim day 100 is treating every high-margin item as free money. Margins are compensation for risk, and each line demands different preparation before the profit is real.

| Product line | Margin potential | Prep required | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | Low to moderate | Testing and filtering | Liability claims from bad batches |
| Hydroponics and organ goods | Steady | Machines plus production time | Slow turnaround ties up capital |
| Weapons | High | Permits, modifications, paperwork | Inspection and confiscation |
| Chemicals | High | Closely guarded recipes | Addiction fallout and raids |
| Fenced and stolen goods | High | A hiding spot and a straight face | Security sweeps |
| Counterfeit relabels | Moderate to high | Strong appraisal instincts | Mob justice and elite retaliation |

Two rules keep this table from ruining you. First, never sell an item you haven't assessed properly — rare goods need real appraisal or you'll overpay, and suppliers routinely overstate value. Second, remember that counterfeiting isn't free money. Relabeling goods as genuine or selling fake product to the arrogant elite works right up until someone with resources decides to make an example of you.

By day 100, most successful shops run one high-margin line for growth and one boring line for stability. The boring line pays the rent; the spicy line pays for licenses and machines.

## Faction Standing, Security Heat, and Rogue AI Checks

Faction work is the quiet multiplier that separates a comfortable Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim day 100 run from a desperate one. Reputation buys access and protection — exclusive deals, better suppliers, and people who warn you before a raid. It also paints a target on your back. On this station, every client is a potential profit and a potential problem, and someone is always taking notes.

### Why Reputation Is a Double-Edged Sword

You can approve a sale, refuse it, report the customer, or look away. Each choice nudges your standing with someone. The trap is trying to stay friendly with everyone: you end up with no allies and no protection when it matters. Pick a lane by mid-run, then protect it.

| Action | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| Bribe your way out of a bust | Keeps your stock and your week intact | Cash drain, and it sets a precedent |
| Report a customer to Security | Security goodwill | Black market friction |
| Look the other way on a shady sale | Underground loyalty | Heat builds quietly in the background |
| Sell counterfeit goods to elites | Excellent margins | Retaliation risk if you're careless |
| Enforce the anti-AI policy | Faction approval across the board | Misreading the signs costs you |

### Reading the Signs Before an Aug Reads You

A sentient AI chip went rogue and took its hosts with it. Augmented citizens look like everyone else — until they don't. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you're the one holding the gun that enforces it. Missing the signs, or misreading them and shooting the wrong customer, both carry a price. Before day 100, get comfortable with the tells and build a habit of watching the counter, not just the inventory screen.

One note on patch drift: community reports point out that the first hotfix adjusted at least one reporting outcome so it no longer damaged black market standing. Check the current patch notes before you build a strategy around a specific reputation interaction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to reach Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim day 100?

It depends entirely on how you play. The game supports a mouse-only option and is explicitly playable without timed input, so you can run long sessions or short ones without pressure. Players who chase licenses and production early tend to move faster because their income scales; players who coast on resale tend to stall out around the middle of a run.

### Do I need black market access to survive to day 100?

No, but it helps enormously. Underground access unlocks better margins and exclusive deals that make the later rent hikes manageable. A cautious, fully legitimate shop can survive, though it usually needs a strong production chain to compensate for thinner margins.

### What happens if I fall behind on rent?

The consequences are practical rather than dramatic. Your store loses attractiveness, fewer customers walk in, revenue drops, and the gap widens. Recovering usually means taking on riskier deals just to stabilize cash flow — which is exactly the moment players get caught with contraband.

### Is day 100 the end of the game?

The overarching goal is to earn enough to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand, so day 100 is better treated as a milestone than a hard finish line. Many players use it as a benchmark: if the shop is stable and the buyout is in sight by then, the run is on track.
