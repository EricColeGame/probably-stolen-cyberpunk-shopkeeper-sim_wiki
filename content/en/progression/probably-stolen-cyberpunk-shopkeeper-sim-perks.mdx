## What Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Perks Actually Are

You roll up the shutter on a shop that isn't really yours yet, and every choice from that moment is a trade-off with a price tag attached. Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim perks don't arrive as a tidy skill tree with glowing nodes — they're the advantages you accumulate by choosing a trade, paying rent on time, and staying useful to the right people. Understanding how those benefits stack is the difference between buying out your shop and getting raided into bankruptcy.

The most important thing to know about Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim perks is that they are situational, not universal. A benefit that makes a fence rich can ruin a water merchant, and a connection that opens one door will paint a target on your back somewhere else on the station. This guide breaks down where the real progression advantages come from and how to sequence them.

## Starting Trade Perks: Choose the Trouble You Monetize

Your opening trade is the single biggest perk decision in the game, because it defines your profit curve *and* your failure mode. The official description lays out six starting points, and each one comes with a distinct package of benefits and hazards.

| Starting path | Core perk (what you gain) | Built-in risk |
|---|---|---|
| Gunsmith | High-value inventory with strong margins | Weapons draw inspections and permit checks |
| Chemist | Recipe-driven products with loyal repeat buyers | Chemicals bring addiction and regulatory heat |
| Water merchant | Steady, universal demand — everyone needs water | Liability when quality slips |
| Fence | Cheap stolen stock and fast turnover | Contraband invites raids and confiscation |
| Farmer | Self-supplied production chain, low input costs | Slower ramp before profits compound |
| Rock bottom | Maximum flexibility, no specialization lock-in | No head start, no safety net |

The key insight here is that you aren't picking a theme — you're picking which kind of trouble you're willing to monetize. Weapons bring permits and modifications. Chemicals depend on closely guarded recipes. Water needs testing and filtration before it's sellable. Contraband just needs a good hiding spot and a straight face, at least until Security notices.

For a first run, the water merchant and farmer paths tend to be the most forgiving perks because demand is constant and the risk is manageable. The fence and gunsmith paths offer the fastest ceiling but demand that you already understand the inspection and bribery systems.

## The Rent-to-Riches Progression Perks

Rent is the metronome that paces every perk in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim. Keep it paid and the game opens up; miss it and confiscated stock can spiral you out of the run. Each milestone you clear unlocks a category of advantage.

| Progression milestone | Perk unlocked | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| First rent cycles paid | Breathing room to reinvest instead of scrambling | Prevents forced fire-sales of inventory |
| Advanced licenses purchased | Access to the underground market | Unlocks higher-margin, higher-risk goods |
| Machines and production chains online | Ability to craft valuable artifacts in-house | Cuts supplier dependency and improves margins |
| Additional jobs mastered | Secondary income (water, hydroponics, organs, and more) | Diversifies against a single product line collapsing |
| Multiple businesses running | Parallel revenue streams | Rent increases stop being existential |
| Faction connections built | Exclusive deals and protected access | Turns reputation into purchasing power |

That table is effectively your perk priority list. Licenses and production chains are the two upgrades that most reliably convert credits into *more* credits, because they remove the middleman who overstates value on every shipment. Multiple businesses are the long-game perk — they're what let you survive a bad deal or a raid without losing the run.

One caveat: every purchase needs to earn its keep. A machine that sits idle is worse than no machine at all, because rent keeps climbing regardless. Player experience from the launch window consistently points to the same trap — over-expanding on credit before a single product line is genuinely profitable.

## Item and Appraisal Perks: Where Margins Are Won

A huge share of your effective perks aren't purchased at all; they're learned. The game rewards players who can assess an item and the person selling it, and it punishes anyone who takes a supplier at their word.

| Item type | Prep perk required | Cost of skipping it |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection before resale | Counterfeit or damaged stock sold at a loss |
| Water | Testing and filtration | Liability claims that eat your margin |
| Weapons | Permits and legal modifications | Inspection seizures |
| Chemicals | Guarded recipes and controlled production | Addiction fallout and enforcement attention |
| Rare goods | Proper appraisal | Overpaying on a misjudged valuation |
| Contraband | Concealment or bribery readiness | Raids, confiscation, mob justice |

Beyond basic handling, there are three "gray" perks that boost margins fast: cutting product quality, relabeling counterfeit goods as genuine, and fencing discounted stolen merchandise. All three are profitable. All three are also the fastest route to a raid if Security reaches you first, and the station has a way of delivering mob justice when official channels fail.

The appraisal perk deserves special mention because it's the one that scales. Suppliers routinely overstate value, and customers push their luck on every haggle. If you can read an item accurately, you keep the margin. If you can read the *person*, you keep the margin and avoid the trap.

## Faction, Reputation, and Risk Perks — Plus the Hidden Costs

Reputation is the most misunderstood system in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim. Perks tied to reputation buy you access and protection, but they also make you visible. Being known is a benefit and a liability in the same breath.

| Customer or faction type | What they offer | What they cost you |
|---|---|---|
| Security informants posing as customers | Bribes, warnings, leniency | Every transaction is being recorded |
| Revolutionaries seeking leverage | Access to restricted goods and contacts | You become a political asset |
| Fallen elites | Premium prices on luxury and counterfeit goods | They test your usefulness, constantly |
| Augs (rogue AI chip hosts) | Nothing but risk | Faction-wide policy says you enforce it — with your gun |
| The mob | Nothing | Everything, if you're caught selling them junk |

The Aug enforcement layer is a genuine progression gate rather than a perk. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you're the one holding the weapon, so misreading the signs carries its own price. Treat it as a skill check you'll be repeating all run, not a one-time story beat.

Community reports from the first week also flag that reputation snowballs in both directions. A shop known for fair appraisals attracts better suppliers; a shop known for counterfeits attracts customers who are looking for a reason to cause trouble.

## How to Prioritize Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Perks Early

If you want a clean sequence for the first several hours, this order works well across most starting trades:

1. **Stabilize rent first.** Never let a rent payment become a coin flip. Keep a buffer before you buy anything optional.
2. **Buy appraisal and inspection habits before upgrades.** Free perks that stop losses are worth more than paid perks that add gains.
3. **Unlock the underground license as your first big purchase.** It's the widest single expansion of your available inventory.
4. **Add one production chain, not three.** A single working machine beats a showroom of idle ones.
5. **Pick a faction and commit.** Spread reputation is thin reputation, and exclusive deals are where the real margins hide.
6. **Only then add a second business.** Diversification is a mid-game perk, not an opening move.

## FAQ

**Do Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim perks work like a skill tree?**
No. There's no conventional perk tree to respec. The progression benefits come from your starting trade, purchased licenses, production upgrades, additional jobs, and faction reputation — each with a matching risk attached.

**Which starting trade has the best perks for a new player?**
Water merchant and farmer are the safest early picks because demand is constant and the failure modes are manageable. Fence and gunsmith have higher ceilings but require you to already understand inspections, permits, and bribery.

**Are the counterfeit and stolen-goods perks worth using?**
They're among the strongest margin boosters in the game, but they're also the fastest way to attract raids and mob justice. Use them on customers who can't retaliate, and keep a bribe fund ready.

**How many Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim perks can I unlock in one run?**
In principle, all of them — the limits are rent pressure and time, not a point budget. In practice, most players specialize in one or two product lines and one faction before expanding into multiple businesses.
