## Quarter One: Surviving the Rent Clock

Your opening months are not about maximizing profit; they are about proving the business can breathe. Pick one product line, learn its inspection rules cold, and resist the urge to diversify before you can cover rent twice over.

| Quarter | Main objective | Cash rule | Biggest threat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (opening months) | Keep rent paid, master one product line | Reinvest only what a bad deal can't wipe out | Overpaying for unappraised stock |
| Q2 | Add a second revenue stream and a first machine | Hold a rent buffer at all times | Inspections and confiscation |
| Q3 | Unlock advanced licenses, court a faction | Spend on access, not vanity stock | Reputation tipping into a target |
| Q4 | Push buyout progress, run multiple businesses | Convert inventory into buyout capital | Rent hikes outpacing margins |

Practical habits for the first quarter:

- Appraise before you buy, every time. Suppliers overstate value, and the cost of a misjudged crate comes straight out of your rent.
- Keep contraband off the shelf until you understand how Security behaves on your station.
- Track which customers actually return. Attracting the right clientele is a supply-and-demand lever, not background flavor.
- Bank one full rent cycle before buying anything optional.

## Choosing a Trade: The Product Line Comparison

The game's opening choice is less about theme and more about which kind of trouble you want to monetize. Weapons bring inspections, chemicals bring addiction, water brings liability, and contraband brings raids. A sensible Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim one year of commerce run usually starts with the calmest curve available and adds volatility later.

| Starting trade | Where the margin comes from | Recurring headache | Good first year? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water merchant | Volume sales after testing and filtering | Liability when quality slips | Yes — steady and teachable |
| Hydroponic farmer | Production chains and crafted goods | Machine upkeep and permits | Yes — slower but safer |
| Gunsmith | Weapon sales and modifications | Permits, inspections, paperwork | Only if you enjoy compliance |
| Chemist | Closely guarded recipes | Addiction fallout, tight regulation | High risk, high reward |
| Fence | Discounted stolen goods | Raids and mob justice | Not recommended for run one |
| Rock bottom | Anything you can afford | No buffer whatsoever | Challenge run territory |

Player experience suggests the biggest early mistake is chasing the fattest margin in month one. The better the margin, the worse the consequence when something goes wrong, and a first-year shop has no reserves to absorb a bad week.

## Mid-Year: Licenses, Machines, and Multiple Businesses

Once rent is routine, expansion becomes a sequencing problem rather than a money problem. Advanced licenses unlock the underground market, machines turn cheap inputs into valuable artifacts, and multiple businesses spread risk — but each one adds a fixed cost that rent will happily eat.

| Priority | Purchase | Why it comes first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rent buffer | A missed payment ends the run faster than thin margins |
| 2 | Appraisal and inspection habits | Stops the slow bleed of overpaying |
| 3 | First production machine | Converts bought goods into crafted, higher-value stock |
| 4 | Advanced license | Opens underground market access and better suppliers |
| 5 | Second business | Diversifies income across product lines |
| 6 | Inventory expansion | Only after everything above is stable |

A Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim one year of commerce plan should also budget for losses. Confiscated stock and one genuinely terrible deal are not edge cases; they are line items. If your mid-year spreadsheet has no allowance for them, the second half of the year will feel much harsher than it needs to.

## Second Half: Factions, Contraband, and Reputation Math

By the back half of the year, your shop is a political object. Every customer could be a security informant gathering evidence, a revolutionary hunting leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you are still useful. You can approve a sale, refuse it, report it, or look away — and someone is always taking notes.

Contraband handling deserves its own checklist, because shortcuts compound:

| Item type | Required step | Shortcut that bites back |
|---|---|---|

| Water | Test and filter | Skipping filtration |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | Moving unpermitted stock |
| Chemicals | Follow the recipe exactly | Cutting product quality |
| Rare items | Proper appraisal | Guessing at value |
| Counterfeit goods | Relabel as genuine (if you dare) | Elite buyers notice |
| Stolen goods | Hide from Security or bribe | Assuming Security won't reach you |

Community reports from the launch window suggest reputation is a double-edged resource: it opens doors to exclusive faction deals while simultaneously making you visible. A healthy Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim one year of commerce strategy usually means one strong ally rather than five lukewarm contacts — depth beats breadth when raids start.

## The Year-End Audit: Did You Buy Out the Shop?

Before you roll into a second year, grade the run honestly. The buyout is the headline goal, but the supporting numbers tell you whether you got there by skill or by luck.

| Metric | Healthy at year end | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|

| Buyout progress | Climbing every quarter | Flat for two consecutive quarters |
| Licenses | At least one advanced tier | None unlocked |

| Contraband exposure | Low and well hidden | Raid risk visibly rising |

If the audit looks shaky, the fix is usually unglamorous: tighten appraisal, cut the riskiest product line, and rebuild the rent buffer before expanding again. A Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim one year of commerce run that ends with a bought-out shop and clean books is worth more than a flashy one that collapses in month eleven.

## FAQ

**How long does one year of commerce take in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim?**
There is no official conversion between in-game time and real hours, and pacing depends on how you play. Treat the year as a structural framework — quarters, rent cycles, and expansion milestones — rather than a fixed clock.

**Can you buy out the shop before the year ends?**
Yes, that is the stated goal, and strong margins plus disciplined appraisals can accelerate it considerably. The risk is overextending on inventory and missing a rent payment on the way.

**Which starting trade is safest for a first Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim one year of commerce run?**
Water merchandising and hydroponic farming are the gentlest entry points because their risks are procedural — testing, filtering, machine upkeep — rather than enforcement-driven. Save gunsmithing, chemistry, and fencing for a second run.

**What happens if contraband gets found?**
The game offers hiding spots and bribery as mitigation, but exposure raises your profile with Security and can cost you stock. Budget for confiscation as a recurring expense, not a one-time accident.
