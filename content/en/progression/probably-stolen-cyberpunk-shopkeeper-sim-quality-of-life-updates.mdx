## Inventory, Appraisal, and Contraband: Where QoL Saves the Most Time

Three systems account for most of the friction in the current build, and each has a manual workaround you can adopt right now.

### Inventory and stock rotation

The inventory loop is where shop sims quietly eat your evening. Player experience suggests a simple discipline: never let a sorting session happen mid-rush. Sort at the start of a session, then trade. Keep a fixed mental slot for contraband that needs hiding, so you are not deciding under pressure when Security shows up.

### Appraisal and counterfeit risk

Rare items need proper appraisal to avoid overpaying, and suppliers routinely overstate value. Until the interface gives you sharper pre-purchase signals, adopt a hard rule: cap your opening offer below your own estimated resale value, and walk away from anything you cannot classify. The margin you protect is worth more than the deal you chase.

### Contraband and reputation

Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back. That trade-off is the game's core tension, and no quality of life update should remove it. What a good update can do is make the risk legible — clearer exposure indicators, clearer raid warnings — so the decision stays hard but stops being opaque.

## A QoL-Friendly Routine You Can Run Today

You do not need a patch to reduce friction. You need a repeatable order of operations that minimizes context switching — the exact thing future **Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim quality of life updates** will automate for you.

- **Open with admin, not sales.** Check rent progress, permits, and machine status before your first customer.
- **Batch your inspection work.** Test water, inspect cigarette packs, and appraise rare goods in one pass.
- **Set a margin floor.** Refuse anything below it, no matter how good the seller's story is.
- **Pay rent early when possible.** A paid rent cycle removes the biggest source of decision pressure.
- **Log your faction moves.** One line per session beats guessing why a supplier went cold.
