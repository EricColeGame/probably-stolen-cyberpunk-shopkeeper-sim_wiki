## Gameplay Deep Dive: Professions, Risk, and Rent

The real question behind any **Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim release date trailer** is whether the systems hold up past the first hour. The answer, based on the game's design, is that your starting profession is essentially a choice of which disaster you'd like to monetize.

| Starting Path | Core Activity | Primary Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Gunsmith | Weapons sales and modifications | Inspections and permit checks |
| Chemist | Profitable chemical production | Addiction fallout, recipe theft |
| Water merchant | Filtering and testing water | Liability claims |
| Fence | Moving stolen and discounted goods | Raids and confiscation |
| Farmer | Hydroponic production chains | Slow margins, equipment cost |
| Rock bottom | No capital, no safety net | Everything, immediately |

Layered on top are the item-level decisions that define your margins. Cigarette packs need visual inspection before resale. Water needs testing and filtration. Rare goods need appraisal so you don't overpay. You can cut quality, relabel counterfeits as genuine, or move stolen stock if you think security won't reach you — but mob justice exists in this game, and it's not decorative.

The rent system is the spine of the whole thing. Keep payments current and you can expand, run multiple businesses, and unlock advanced licenses for the underground market. Fall behind and confiscated stock can spiral you into a death loop. Every purchase has to earn its keep.

A few practical tips from the design itself:

- **Don't over-diversify early.** Each product line carries its own compliance risk; stacking three at once multiplies inspection exposure before you have the cash buffer.
- **Budget rent first, inventory second.** Rising rent is the only loss condition that never stops.
- **Bribe before you're caught, not after.** Getting caught with contraband costs more than the bribe ever would.
- **Watch customers, not just items.** Security informants pay like anyone else — right up until they don't.
