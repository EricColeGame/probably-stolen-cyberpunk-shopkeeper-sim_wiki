## The Core Loop: Picking Your Kind of Trouble

Here's the design idea the trailer communicates in a single line without ever saying it outright: you aren't choosing a shop theme, you're choosing which consequences you can absorb. Weapons bring inspections. Chemicals bring addiction. Water brings liability. Contraband brings raids. The better the margin, the worse the fallout when something slips.

| Starting path | Profit ceiling | Primary risk |
|---|---|---|
| Gunsmith | High | Permit checks and inspections |
| Chemist | High | Addiction mechanics and guarded recipes |
| Water merchant | Steady | Testing, filtering, and liability |
| Fence | Volatile | Raids and mob justice |
| Hydroponic farmer | Low but stable | Slow scaling, machinery costs |
| Rock bottom | Lowest | You start with almost nothing |

Every item you stock carries its own verification step. Cigarette packs need a visual inspection before resale. Water has to be tested and filtered. Weapons require permits and modifications. Rare goods need appraisal so you don't overpay. You can also cut quality, relabel counterfeits as genuine, or move discounted stolen goods — provided you're confident Security won't reach you first. Suppliers overstate value, customers push their luck, and contraband often arrives with problems you only discover after the deal closes.

Haggling sits on top of all of it. The trailer shows a handshake and a smirk; the game shows a trade-off. Winning a negotiation usually means accepting a different kind of cost, whether that's a lower-quality batch, a favor owed, or a customer who now knows your name.

## Customers, Factions, and the Aug Check

The trailer's most striking beat is the aug scan, and it's the one that separates Probably Stolen from the cozy-shopkeeper genre it superficially resembles. A sentient AI chip went rogue and took its hosts with it, and augmented citizens look exactly like everyone else until they don't. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you're the one holding the gun that enforces it. Miss the signs, or misread them, and there's a price.

| Customer type | What they want | Why they're dangerous |
|---|---|---|
| Security fisher | Evidence of contraband | Reporting you is the point |
| Revolutionary | Leverage over factions | Your stock becomes ammunition |
| Fallen elite | To test your usefulness | Will report you the moment you stop being useful |
| Augmented citizen | Goods, quietly | Misreading the signs carries its own penalty |
| Ordinary buyer | A fair price | Still reports counterfeit goods |

Reputation is the connective tissue. It buys access and protection — better suppliers, exclusive faction deals, advanced licenses that open the underground market — but it also paints a target. Building connections with the right faction means making enemies of another, and the trailer conveys that tension by never showing a clean win.

The rest of the loop is quieter than the trailer lets on. Machines and production chains let you craft valuable artifacts instead of just reselling them. Additional jobs like organ dealer or hydroponic farmer expand your operation. You can run multiple businesses and unlock new merchandise lines, but rent keeps rising, and a confiscated shipment can wipe out the payment you needed.

## Trailer vs. Reality — and First-Shift Tips

The launch trailer is honest about tone and selective about detail. Here's what it skips.

| Trailer implies | The full game adds |
|---|---|
| Fast, cinematic sales | Inventory tetris and stock rotation |
| A single shop counter | Machine management and production chains |
| Getting caught is dramatic | Bribery, hiding goods, and permit paperwork |
| Clean moral choices | Reputation costs that compound across factions |
| One storefront | Multiple businesses and license progression |

For players starting now, a few practical notes:

- **Pick a low-volatility trade first.** Water or hydroponics gives you a stable base to learn appraisal and haggling before raids become a concern.
- **Verify before you mark up.** Testing water and inspecting packs costs time but protects your reputation, which is the resource that unlocks better suppliers.
- **Pay rent early, not last.** The rent clock is the real antagonist; a confiscated shipment the day before payment is how runs end.
- **Track faction standing deliberately.** Spreading favors evenly sounds safe and usually isn't — exclusive deals require commitment.
- **Watch the aug tells.** Misreading a host is cheaper than ignoring one, and both are cheaper than guessing wrong in front of Security.

If you want the systems explained in motion, the [Steam store page's trailer and screenshot gallery](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4348910/Probably_Stolen_Cyberpunk_Shopkeeper_Sim/) is the best reference — it's also where the accessibility options (adjustable text size, mouse-only play, no required quick-time events) are listed, which matters if you're deciding between this and a more input-heavy sim.
