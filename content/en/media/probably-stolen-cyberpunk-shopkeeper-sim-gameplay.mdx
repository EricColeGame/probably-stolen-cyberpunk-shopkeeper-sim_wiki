## Picking a Starting Trade: Six Paths, Six Kinds of Trouble

Your opening choice is less about theme and more about which disaster you are comfortable monetizing. Weapons bring inspections. Chemicals bring addiction. Water brings liability. Contraband brings raids. None of these paths is "safe" — they simply fail differently.

| Starting trade | Main profit driver | Signature risk | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gunsmith | Weapon sales, permits, and modifications | Random inspections and confiscation | Players who enjoy tense paperwork |
| Chemist | High-margin chemicals made from guarded recipes | Addiction fallout and raids | Risk-tolerant traders |
| Water merchant | Steady volume sales | Liability claims if testing is skipped | Players who want reliable cash flow |
| Fence | Discounted stolen goods | Security attention and mob justice | High-risk, high-reward operators |
| Hydroponic farmer | Production chains and crafted goods | Slow early ramp | Patient builders |
| Rock bottom | Nothing but grit and a straight face | Everything, immediately | Veterans on a second run |

A practical note for new players: your first trade should be the one whose failure state you can recognize quickly. A water merchant learns the inspection rhythm fast because the mistakes are obvious. A fence learns nothing until the raid happens. If you are still learning how the appraisal screen behaves, start with volume and move into contraband later once you have a cash cushion.

## The Core Loop: Inspect, Appraise, Haggle, Sell

Every item you stock carries its own verification ritual, and skipping that ritual is how runs die. The game gives you real tools — visual inspection, filtration, permits, recipe knowledge, and appraisal — and then punishes you for being in a hurry.

| Item type | Required step | What goes wrong if you skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection before resale | Counterfeits sold as genuine, reputation hit |
| Water | Test and filter | Liability complaints from buyers |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | Stock confiscated during inspection |
| Profitable chemicals | Closely guarded recipes | Quality collapse and addiction consequences |
| Rare goods | Careful appraisal | Overpaying on a supplier's inflated valuation |

Suppliers overstate value. Customers push their luck. Contraband often arrives with problems you only discover after the deal closes, which means your margin is really a measure of how well you assessed what was in front of you. Misjudge the item or the seller, and you absorb the loss yourself.

### Counterfeit, Cut, and Fence: The Three Dirty Levers

Once you understand the honest version of the loop, the game hands you three levers that bend it:

- **Cut quality** to reduce costs, accepting reputation damage when buyers notice.
- **Relabel counterfeit goods as genuine** to charge premium prices to the arrogant elite.
- **Fence stolen merchandise** at a discount, betting that Security will not connect it back to you.

Each lever is a genuine strategy, not a trap. The problem is that all three compound. A shop known for cheap fakes attracts exactly the customers who file complaints, and complaints attract the factions that keep notes.

## Factions, Augs, and Reputation: Who's Across the Counter

Reputation in this game is not a single meter — it is a set of relationships that buy you access and protection while simultaneously painting a target on your back. Approve a sale, refuse it, report it, or look away; someone is always recording the outcome.

| Counter encounter | What they want | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Security informant | Evidence of contraband | Reporting earns goodwill; selling earns heat |
| Revolutionary | Leverage against the station's power structure | Connections unlock exclusive deals |
| Fallen elite | Proof you are still useful | Prime target for counterfeit sales — and revenge |
| Augmented customer | To pass as ordinary | You are expected to spot the signs and act |

That last category deserves emphasis. A sentient AI chip went rogue and took its hosts with it, and augs look like everyone else until they very clearly do not. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you are the one holding the gun that enforces it. Missing the signs — or misreading them and acting on a hunch — carries its own price, which makes this the tensest interaction type in the game.

## Rent, Expansion, and Practical Survival Tips

Rent is the real antagonist. It rises on a schedule you do not control, and a single confiscated shipment or bad appraisal can leave you scrambling for the next payment. Survive long enough and you can expand the operation, run multiple businesses, and unlock new merchandise and production opportunities — but every purchase has to earn its keep.

| Phase | Main focus | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Early game | Pay rent, learn appraisal, build a small cash buffer | Overpaying suppliers on rare goods |
| Mid game | Unlock advanced licenses, add a second product line | Confiscation wiping out inventory value |
| Late game | Multiple businesses and underground market access | Rent scaling faster than your margins |

A few habits that make the early hours much smoother:

- **Inspect before you buy, not after.** The appraisal screen exists to save you money, and it is cheaper than a reputation repair.
- **Keep one clean product line.** A legal, reliable seller funds your riskier experiments.
- **Hide contraband properly, and budget for bribes.** Getting caught is survivable if you have cash set aside.
- **Treat reputation as an asset with a maintenance cost.** Access and protection are worth more than one inflated sale.
- **Track rent before you upgrade machines.** Production chains are great until they cost you a payment.

## FAQ

**Is Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim gameplay focused on trading or combat?**
Trading and appraisal drive nearly everything. The weapon you carry exists for enforcing the station's augmentation policy, and those moments are judgment calls rather than action sequences. Your economy is the real battlefield.

**Can I play it on a Steam Deck?**
Yes. Valve rates the game as Playable on Steam Deck, and the mouse-only option plus adjustable text size make it comfortable on smaller screens.

**Does it support multiplayer or co-op?**
No. It is a single-player experience, though it supports Steam Cloud saves and Family Sharing, so household members can share a library copy.

**Is it beginner friendly for someone new to shop sims?**
Generally yes. Starting as a water merchant or gunsmith teaches the inspection loop with clear feedback, and you can move into fencing and counterfeits once you understand how suppliers and customers try to mislead you. The main adjustment for newcomers is accepting that a bad appraisal costs real money — there is no undo button on a signed deal.
