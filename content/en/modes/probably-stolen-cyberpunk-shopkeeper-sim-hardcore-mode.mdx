A **Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim hardcore mode** run is what happens when you deliberately remove that cushion and force every transaction to matter.

## Pick a Starting Trade That Survives the Squeeze

Your opening trade is the single biggest hardcore decision, because it decides which failure mode you're signing up for. The game lets you begin as a gunsmith, chemist, water merchant, fence, farmer, or from absolute rock bottom — and each path has a different profit curve and a different way of ruining you.

Weapons bring inspections. Chemicals bring addiction and controlled recipes. Water brings liability. Contraband brings raids. Farming is slow but stable. Starting from rock bottom is the purest hardcore opening and also the least forgiving, since you have no inventory buffer to absorb a bad buy.

| Starting trade | Margin profile | Hardcore viability | Primary threat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Farmer | Low, steady | Excellent for learners | Boredom-driven overexpansion |
| Water merchant | Thin, volume-based | Strong | Testing and filtering liability |
| Chemist | High, volatile | Strong for confident players | Recipe secrecy and addiction fallout |
| Gunsmith | High, regulated | Moderate | Permit and modification audits |
| Fence | Spiky, opportunistic | Expert only | Raids and mob justice |
| Rock bottom | None | Masochist tier | Everything, immediately |

Community reports consistently point to farming or water as the sanest hardcore start: both give you a repeatable inspection loop you can practice before you touch anything with a permit attached. If you want the full suffer experience, rock bottom is right there.

## The Rent Clock Is Your Real Difficulty Slider

Every run in Probably Stolen is a race. You're earning enough to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand — and rent climbs whether or not your margins keep pace. In a hardcore ruleset, the rent clock is the only difficulty setting you actually need, because it converts every discretionary purchase into a risk.

The trap is expansion. The game explicitly lets you run multiple businesses, unlock new merchandise, and build production chains, and all of it is genuinely profitable — later. Buy a machine or a license too early and you've converted liquid capital into a fixed cost that rent will happily eat.

| Phase | What you're optimizing | Hardcore target | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Learning one product line cold | Positive margin on every single sale | Buying stock you can't inspect |
| Squeeze | Cash buffer vs. rent | Buffer equal to several rent cycles | Selling at cost to make rent |
| Expansion | Machines and licenses | Expansion funded only by profit | Dipping into rent money |
| Buyout | Buying out the shop | Debt-free, no contraband on premises | Raid risk during the final stretch |

A practical hardcore habit: before any purchase above your normal stock budget, ask whether it survives two bad deals in a row. If the answer is no, you're gambling with rent money, and rent doesn't negotiate.

## Contraband, Counterfeits, and Appraisal Disasters

This is where hardcore runs die. The game's core tension is that every item carries its own risk, and the margins depend entirely on how well you assess what's in front of you — both the goods and the person selling them. Suppliers overstate value. Customers push their luck. Contraband arrives with problems you only notice after the deal is closed.

You can cut a product's quality, relabel counterfeit goods as genuine, or fence discounted stolen merchandise if you believe Security won't reach you. In hardcore, that belief needs to be earned rather than assumed, because confiscated stock doesn't just cost you the item — it costs you the rent payment that item was supposed to cover.

| Item type | Required step | Hardcore rule | Cost of skipping |

### A hardcore daily checklist

- Inspect every incoming item before it touches the shelf.
- Confirm the day's rent coverage before any discretionary spend.
- Log which faction each regular customer leans toward.
- Watch for aug tells on unfamiliar faces; flag before you serve.
- Verify contraband is stashed before closing, every single day.
- Skip the counterfeit relabel unless the buyer is an elite and a one-time customer.

## FAQ

**Does Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim hardcore mode exist as an official setting?**
Based on the official store material, no — there's no labeled hardcore difficulty option listed. Players construct it themselves using self-imposed restrictions like single-save runs, no-reload rules, and mandatory inspection on every purchase.

**What's the best starting trade for a hardcore run?**
Farming and water merchandising are the most forgiving hardcore openers because they teach the inspection loop cheaply. Fence and rock-bottom starts are expert-tier, since a single raid or bad appraisal can end the run outright.

**How do I survive rising rent in a hardcore ruleset?**
Treat rent as a fixed cost you pre-fund, not a bill you pay from whatever's left. Keep a buffer worth several rent cycles, expand only from profit, and never buy stock you can't fully inspect before resale.

**Can I still sell counterfeits and stolen goods in hardcore mode?**
Yes, but ration it. Sell counterfeits to elites as one-time transactions, keep exactly one stash location, and always hold back enough capital to bribe your way out — otherwise mob justice or Security will finish the run for you.

Hardcore mode in Probably Stolen isn't a menu option — it's a discipline. Master one product line, protect the rent buffer, read every customer twice, and the buyout stops being a fantasy. Get greedy on day three, and the station will happily remind you who's really in charge.
