## What the Model Citizen Playstyle Actually Asks of You

Instead of fencing stolen rifles and bribing inspectors, you play the shopkeeper who checks the paperwork, flags the contraband, and hands the Augs over to Security. It matters because the clean route is one of the most stable ways to survive rising rent, and almost every guide online ignores it in favor of quick black-market profits.

Here's the honest framing: Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Model Citizen mode isn't a separate campaign bolted onto the game. It's a self-imposed code of conduct layered over the existing shop sim. You still haggle, still craft, still chase margins — you just do it without poisoning your record. Every sale is still a gamble, because the person across the counter could be a Security informant, a revolutionary hunting leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you're useful.

What separates the two approaches comes down to where your money and your risk live.

| Dimension | Model Citizen Playstyle | Black Market Fence |

The trade-off is real: clean runs grow slowly, while contraband runs spike fast and then collapse when an inspection lands at the wrong moment. If you want a run that survives past the first few rent hikes, the slow lane is usually the smarter bet.

## Reputation, Reporting, and the Security Tightrope

Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back. That tension is the heart of the clean playstyle. Reporting a suspicious customer can raise your standing with Security, yet it can also cost you underworld contacts you'll want later — and community reports note that the first hotfix specifically changed how reporting one character, Lou, affects Black Market reputation, which clean players welcomed.

The trick is to treat reporting as a resource, not a reflex.

| Situation | Report It? | Upside | Hidden Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer shows clear Aug tells | Yes | Security goodwill, safer station | Possible loss of a repeat buyer |
| Courier hauling obvious contraband | Usually | Confiscation credit, faction trust | Suppliers may blacklist you |
| Desperate first-time offender | Rarely | Nothing meaningful | Reputation damage, no payoff |
| Rival shopkeeper skimming | Sometimes | Leverage with authorities | Escalation you may not want |
| High-value elite testing you | Carefully | Access to exclusive deals | Backlash from powerful clients |

Every faction agrees on the policy toward rogue Augs, and you're the one holding the gun that enforces it. But misreading the signs carries its own price, and accusing a legitimate customer is a fast way to lose foot traffic you can't easily rebuild.

## Spotting Augs and Handling Contraband

Augs look like everyone else until they don't. Community players describe the tells as small inconsistencies — a pause that lands wrong, a credential that doesn't match the story, a reaction that's a beat too slow. In Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Model Citizen mode, your job is to notice those beats without turning the shop into an interrogation room.

Contraband handling is where clean runs are won or lost, because the game lets you cut quality, relabel counterfeits as genuine, and fence discounted stolen goods if you think Security won't reach you. The clean answer is usually the boring one.

| Item Category | Required Step Before Sale | Clean-Run Move | Risk If Skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection | Inspect, then sell as-is | Fines, damaged reputation |
| Water | Testing and filtering | Filter every batch | Liability claims |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | License fully, refuse off-book work | Inspections, confiscation |
| Chemicals | Closely guarded recipes | Stick to licensed recipes | Addiction fallout, raids |

Suppliers overstate value, customers push their luck, and contraband often arrives with problems you only spot after the credits change hands. Your margin is really a measure of how well you assess what's in front of you.

## Making Rent Without Breaking the Rules

Rent climbs over time no matter how good your week was. Fall behind and the consequences are mechanical, not cosmetic: your store loses attractiveness, fewer customers walk in, revenue drops, and the gap widens. That spiral is what pushes most players into risky deals just to stabilize cash flow.

The clean counter-strategy is to build several small, reliable levers instead of one big swing.

| Lever | Action | Cost | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer mix | Stock what attracts the right buyers | Inventory capital | Steadier daily traffic |
| Authentication | Appraise before purchase | Time and tools | Fewer bad buys |
| Haggling | Push for better terms, accept trade-offs | Relationship risk | Higher per-sale margin |
| Production chains | Craft valuable artifacts from raw inputs | Machinery, licenses | Best long-term margins |
| Faction connections | Build ties for exclusive deals | Loyalty commitments | Access to restricted stock |
| Inventory management | Expand storage, rotate slow stock | Upfront spend | Less dead capital |

One caution: selling counterfeit goods to arrogant elites is tempting and genuinely profitable, but it's the single fastest way to break a clean run. If the goal is Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Model Citizen mode, skip it — the reputational cost outlives the payout.

## Common Mistakes That Break a Clean Run

Even experienced shopkeepers fumble the law-abiding path. These are the recurring ones:

- **Reporting everything that moves.** Accusation fatigue drains your customer base faster than contraband ever would.
- **Skipping appraisals to save time.** Overpaying once can cost more than a full week of rent.
- **Ignoring the rent curve.** Rent doesn't care how your week went, so build the cushion before you need it.
- **Overinvesting in one expensive item.** If it's confiscated, fails inspection, or simply doesn't sell, you may not cover what you owe.
- **Treating Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Model Citizen mode as a restriction instead of a strategy.** The point isn't purity — it's predictable margins you can plan around.
- **Forgetting licenses.** Advanced licenses are what let you access better markets without slipping underground.

## FAQ

**Is Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Model Citizen mode an official difficulty setting?**
Treat it as a playstyle rather than a menu toggle. The game's systems — reporting, reputation, inspections, and faction politics — fully support a law-abiding run, but the constraint is one you impose on yourself. That's also why it's flexible: you decide how strictly you enforce it.

**Can you actually make money without selling contraband?**
Yes, just more slowly. Water, hydroponics, licensed gunsmithing, and authenticated resale all produce reliable income, and production chains eventually outperform smuggling because they don't carry raid risk.

**Should you report every Aug you suspect?**
No. Reporting is a resource with real costs. Flag clear cases with obvious tells, and let ambiguous ones walk — a false accusation costs you a customer and sometimes a faction contact.

**What's the best starting profession for a first clean run?**
Hydroponic farmer or water merchant. Both keep your compliance burden low, generate repeat traffic, and leave room to expand into licensed goods once your cash cushion exists.

**Does the playstyle hold up long term?**
Community reports suggest it does, especially after early patches smoothed out reputation penalties for reporting. The ceiling is lower than a smuggling run, but the floor is far higher — and in a game about paying rent, the floor is what keeps the shutter open.
