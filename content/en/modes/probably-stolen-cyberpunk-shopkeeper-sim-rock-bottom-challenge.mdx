## What the Rock Bottom Start Actually Gives You

Opening a shop with nothing but nerve and a bad reputation is the fastest way to learn how Probably Stolen really works. The Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Rock Bottom challenge strips away the safety net of a stocked backroom and forces you to earn every credit across the counter. If you have already run a comfortable gunsmith or chemist build and want the station to feel dangerous again, the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Rock Bottom challenge is the mode that will do it.

The game launched on October 28, 2026 from developer Questing Goose Studio and publisher tinyBuild, and it has settled at an Overwhelmingly Positive rating on Steam. Most players pick a trade on day one. A stubborn minority picks nothing at all and dares the rent clock to catch them.

That is the whole appeal. Probably Stolen is a shopkeeping sim set on a dystopian space station where you craft, trade, and smuggle goods of questionable origin, balance faction loyalties, and try to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand. Choosing "absolute rock bottom" as your opening is really choosing the steepest possible version of that loop.

Community reports describe the opening as the leanest in the game: almost no starting capital, no established product line to fall back on, and no supplier relationship worth the name. You are not inheriting a business. You are inheriting a counter, a debt, and a queue of people who can smell desperation.

## Why the Rock Bottom Challenge Hurts So Good

Every other start gives you a product identity. The gunsmith knows what the shop is. The water merchant knows what the shop is. Rock Bottom gives you a question instead: what can you afford to become this week?

That forced flexibility is the best teacher in the game. You learn price appraisal because overpaying once can end a run. You learn the visual inspection minigames because a bad batch of cigarettes or an unfiltered water crate will wipe out your margin. You learn faction politics because the only cheap supplier you can reach is somebody's enemy.

There is also a hidden tutorial buried in the misery. Players who survive the first stretch of a Rock Bottom run tend to sail through the standard starts, because they have already internalized the two rules that matter: never spend your last credits on inventory you cannot move, and never let the rent counter become a surprise.

One more thing makes this start memorable. The station's aug detection mechanic — spotting hosts carrying a rogue AI chip — becomes genuinely tense when you cannot afford to lose a single customer. Missing the signs or misreading them carries a price, and on Rock Bottom that price is often the whole run.

## The Rules: Building Your Own Rock Bottom Challenge

There is no separate scoring screen for this. The Rock Bottom start is official; the "challenge" layer is player-made. What follows is a three-tier ruleset assembled from player experience write-ups and community reports, so treat it as a template rather than gospel.

| Tier 2 — No Safety Net | Tier 1 plus: no bribing Security, no selling counterfeit goods, no accepting loans or faction handouts. | Players who have finished one full shop buyout |
| Tier 3 — Nightmare Counter | Tier 2 plus: no reloading a bad deal, one save file, and you must accept every customer who reaches the counter. | Veterans chasing a story worth telling |

Tier 3 is where the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Rock Bottom challenge turns into a horror game. Accepting every customer means accepting the security fisher looking for evidence and the fallen elite testing whether you are worth the trouble. Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back, and you cannot dodge the paint.

Pick your tier before you load the save. Deciding mid-run that a rule was "optional" is how challenge runs quietly become normal runs.

## First Shifts: How to Not Go Bankrupt

Your opening hours are a cash-flow problem, not a profit problem. Profit comes later, once you have a product line and a supplier who does not laugh at your offer. Right now you need turnover, and you need it before the rent timer gets loud.

Practical order of operations for the opening stretch:

- **Buy the smallest viable batch.** Two or three units of one item you understand beats ten units of something you have never appraised.
- **Inspect everything before you resell.** Cigarette packs need a visual check. Water needs testing and filtering. Skipping the check is how a cheap crate becomes an expensive lesson.
- **Haggle, but know your walk-away number.** Every bargain carries a trade-off, and the trade-off is usually quality, delivery time, or a favor you will owe later.
- **Keep a rent buffer.** Whatever your next payment is, hold enough liquid credits to cover it before you restock.
- **Say no to the first obvious trap.** The deal that looks too good in hour one is the deal that gets your stock confiscated in hour six.

| Priority | Why it matters | When you can skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Rent buffer | Confiscated stock or one bad deal can leave you short | Never — this is the run-ending failure state |
| Cheap, fast-moving stock | Generates the turnover that funds everything else | Once a production chain is running |
| Basic appraisal tools | Stops you overpaying on rare goods | Never, if you plan to buy from strangers |
| Storage and hiding space | Contraband needs somewhere to live during inspections | If you are running a clean Tier 2 build |
| Machines and licenses | Unlocks better margins and the underground market | Until your first rent payment clears |

## Reputation, Factions, and the Cost of Cutting Corners

The temptation on Rock Bottom is to take every shortcut the game offers. Cutting a product's quality, relabeling counterfeit goods as genuine, and fencing stolen merchandise all improve margins immediately. They also stack risk, and the risk does not stay abstract.

| Shortcut | Immediate upside | What eventually comes for you |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting product quality | Higher margin per unit | Repeat customers stop returning |
| Relabeling counterfeits as genuine | Elite buyers pay full price | Reputation hit when the fraud surfaces |
| Fencing stolen goods | Cheap inventory, fast turnover | Raids and Security attention |
| Bribing your way out | Avoids one bad night | Costs scale; the paper trail grows |
| Selling to the wrong faction | Quick sale, no questions | Faction relationships close doors permanently |

The smarter Rock Bottom play is to pick one gray-area trade and get excellent at it rather than dabbling in all of them. A fence who understands Security patrol timing is survivable. A fence who also cuts chemicals and lies to elites is a raid waiting for a date.

Mob justice is real here too. The station's customers keep notes, and a shopkeeper who cheats the poor and flatters the rich eventually runs out of both.

## Common Mistakes That End Rock Bottom Runs

Most failed attempts at the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Rock Bottom challenge die from the same handful of errors. Watch for these:

- **Overbuying on the first good offer.** A single supplier who overstates value can eat your entire float.
- **Ignoring the aug check.** Augs look like everyone else until they do not, and a missed identification can cost you the encounter.
- **Expanding before stabilizing.** Running multiple businesses is a late-game reward, not an opening move. Every purchase needs to earn its keep.
- **Treating reputation as a score.** It is a currency with a downside — visibility attracts the wrong kind of attention.
- **Forgetting that rent rises.** The number that was manageable last cycle is not the number you will pay next cycle.

If you want a single rule to carry into every attempt, make it this: on Rock Bottom, the goal is not to get rich fast. It is to still be open next week.

## FAQ

**Is the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Rock Bottom challenge an official game mode?**
The starting option is official — the game lets you begin as a gunsmith, chemist, water merchant, fence, farmer, or from absolute rock bottom. The challenge rules layered on top are self-imposed by players, which is why different communities run slightly different versions.

**How long does a Rock Bottom run take before it gets easier?**
Player experience suggests the first few rent cycles are the filter. Once you have a reliable product line, a rent buffer, and one faction contact who will sell to you without gouging, the run shifts from survival to planning.

**Can you still buy out your shop starting from nothing?**
Yes, and that is the point of the run. The buyout is the win condition regardless of your starting path — the Rock Bottom start just removes the cushion and makes every bad appraisal matter more.

**What should I buy first on a Rock Bottom start?**
Cheap, fast-moving stock you can inspect and flip, plus enough left over to cover the next rent payment. Skip machines, licenses, and expansion until the basics are stable.
