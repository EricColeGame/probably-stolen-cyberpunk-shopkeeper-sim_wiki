## What the Water Trader Challenge Is (and Isn't)

Probably Stolen — developed by Questing Goose Studio and published by tinyBuild — is a cyberpunk shopkeeping simulator set on a dystopian space station. You craft, trade, and smuggle goods of questionable origin, juggle faction loyalties, and try to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand. The game launched on Windows on October 28, 2026, and sits at 96% positive from 656 Steam reviews in its opening window.

When you start a new shop you pick a trade: gunsmith, chemist, water merchant, fence, farmer, or absolute rock bottom. The Water Trader challenge simply takes the water merchant lane and refuses to let you escape it. No pivoting to weapons when rent spikes. No quiet fencing on the side unless your variant allows it.

None of these are official modes. They're agreements you make with yourself, which is exactly why the run is so revealing — the only enforcement is your own discipline.

## Why Water Is the Sneakiest Product Line on the Station

Every product line in Probably Stolen has its own way of ruining you. Weapons bring inspections and permit checks. Chemicals bring addiction and closely guarded recipes. Contraband brings raids. Water, according to the developers' own description, brings **liability** — and liability is the quietest way to bleed a shop dry.

Water also has to be tested and filtered before it's worth selling. That means your margin isn't the difference between buy price and sell price. It's the difference between buy price, testing time, filter upkeep, and whatever a bad batch costs you in refunds and reputation.

| Product line | Signature risk | Upfront cost | Fit for this challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | Liability, purity testing, filtration | Low | The core of the run |
| Weapons | Permits, inspections, modifications | High | Usually banned in strict variants |
| Chemicals | Addiction, guarded recipes | Medium | Banned in clean variants |
| Fenced / stolen goods | Security sweeps, raids | Variable | Emergency-only or banned |
| Hydroponic crops | Machine upkeep, grow time | Medium | Common support line |

The reason the challenge works is volume. Water is cheap per unit, so you need throughput to matter — and throughput means more customers, more haggling, more chances to misjudge a face across the counter.

## Your First Ten Days: The Repeatable Water Loop

The Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Water Trader challenge rewards routine over cleverness. Build a loop, run it every day, and only break it when something forces your hand.

| Phase | What you do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Buy from suppliers, but appraise before you commit | Suppliers overstate value; misjudging the seller means you absorb the cost |
| Test | Check every batch before it hits the shelf | Untested water is a liability waiting to trigger |
| Filter | Run and maintain your filtration equipment | Skipping this is the most common way runs die |
| Price | Set margins with haggling room built in | Every bargain has a trade-off; don't give away the farm |
| Sell | Read the customer, not just the offer | Security fishers, revolutionaries, and fallen elites all behave differently |
| Bank | Move a fixed slice into a rent reserve | Rent rises over time regardless of how your week went |

Two habits separate a run that lasts from one that collapses in week three. First, never sell a batch you haven't tested, even when the queue is long — a liability event costs more than the sales you missed. Second, keep a rent reserve that you do not touch for inventory, no matter how good a deal looks.

A practical daily rhythm looks like this:

- Open with a machine check so filtration never surprises you mid-rush.
- Buy in small lots until a supplier has proven reliable across several deals.
- Test and filter in batches to keep throughput steady.
- Reserve the last part of the day for restocking and bookkeeping, not new deals.
- Log which customer types showed up so you can attract the right ones tomorrow.

## Pricing, Filtration, and the Real Cost of a Liter

New water traders usually price against the sticker on the crate. Experienced ones price against a stack of costs that never appear on the label.

| Cost layer | What it consumes | How to keep it small |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | Upfront capital per crate | Appraise rare or unusual goods before buying; walk away from vague sellers |
| Testing | Time and consumables | Batch test; never spot-check a whole shipment |
| Filtration | Equipment wear and upkeep | Maintain machines before failure, not after |
| Liability | Refunds, reputation damage, security attention | Keep records, refuse sketchy resellers, don't relabel quality |
| Rent | A rising fixed cost you can't haggle | Set the reserve percentage on day one and treat it as untouchable |

There's also the temptation to cut quality — thinning a product or relabeling counterfeit stock as genuine. In a water run, that's the fastest way to convert a manageable margin into an unmanageable liability. The elite customers may never notice. Security might. Mob justice usually does.

One more pricing lever: haggling. The game explicitly warns that every bargain comes with a trade-off. In practice, that means the discount you grant today often returns as a customer who expects it forever, or as a reputation shift you didn't plan for. Decide your floor before the conversation starts.

## Mistakes That Sink a Run — and How to Fix Them

Most failed attempts at the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Water Trader challenge don't end in a dramatic raid. They end in a slow slide: the store loses attractiveness, fewer customers walk in, revenue drops, and the gap to rent widens. Recovering from that slide usually means taking riskier deals just to stabilize cash flow — which is exactly how a clean run becomes a dirty one.

| Mistake | What actually happens | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping filtration to save time | A liability event erases several days of profit | Treat filtration as a fixed cost, not an optional step |
| Chasing volume with no reserve | Rent gap widens even in a good sales week | Bank the reserve before you restock |
| Trusting a supplier's appraisal | You overpay for stock you can't move | Appraise anything unusual; haggle from knowledge |
| Taking risky deals to cover a shortfall | One confiscation ends the run | Cut expenses and shrink inventory instead |
| Ignoring who's at the counter | Reputation damage or a security visit | Watch for the signs; not every customer is a customer |

Don't forget the station's other threat. A rogue AI chip took its hosts with it, and augmented citizens look like everyone else until they don't. Every faction agrees on the policy, and you're the one holding the gun that enforces it. Misreading those signs — or missing them entirely — carries its own price, challenge run or not.

Reputation is the counterweight to all of this. It buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back. Community reports note that a post-launch hotfix changed how reporting a character named Lou to Security affects Black Market reputation, so if your variant tracks faction standing closely, verify the behavior in your current build rather than trusting older guides.

## FAQ

**Is the Water Trader challenge an official game mode?**
No. Probably Stolen ships with starting professions, including water merchant, but the challenge is a community-framed ruleset built on top of that start. You define the restrictions — no contraband, no counterfeit relabeling, no bribes — and you enforce them.

**How do you make water profitable when the margins are so thin?**
Volume plus discipline. Test and filter every batch so liability never eats a week of sales, price with haggling room built in, and keep a rent reserve that inventory purchases can't touch. Attracting the right customers matters as much as the price tag.

**Can you mix water trading with other jobs?**
Some variants allow it. Hydroponic farming pairs naturally with a water operation because both lean on machines and production chains. What most players ban is the escape hatch — pivoting to weapons or fenced goods the moment rent gets uncomfortable, which defeats the purpose of the run.

**What's the fastest way to fail the Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim Water Trader challenge?**
Skipping filtration to keep up with demand. It looks like efficiency for a few days, then a liability event lands, reputation dips, foot traffic falls, and you're taking bad deals to cover rent. The second fastest is trusting a supplier's valuation on a shipment you never appraised.

Run it once with the Clean Ledger rules and you'll never look at a crate of water the same way again.
