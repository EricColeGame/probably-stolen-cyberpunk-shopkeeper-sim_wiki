## Why Everyone Searches for Difficulty Settings Before Opening the Shutters

The rent is already climbing, the customer across the counter might be a security informant, and your stock is technically evidence. That's the opening pitch of Questing Goose Studio's dystopian storefront sim — and it's exactly why players start hunting for **Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim difficulty settings** before they even stock their first shelf. The honest answer surprises people: this isn't a game with a clean Easy/Normal/Hard menu waiting in the options screen. Difficulty in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim is baked into the choices you make before the first sale — your starting profession, your product line, your faction allegiances, and how greedy you get with counterfeit goods.

This guide breaks down every lever that actually changes how hard the game feels, ranks the starting paths from forgiving to brutal, and gives you concrete ways to dial the pressure up or down mid-run.

## Do Traditional Difficulty Settings Exist in Probably Stolen?

Based on the official Steam store page at launch, Probably Stolen ships without a conventional difficulty selector. There's no slider labeled "Casual / Standard / Nightmare," and the accessibility options listed focus on adjustable text size, custom volume controls, and a mouse-only input option — quality-of-life features rather than challenge modifiers.

That's not a design flaw; it's a design philosophy. The developers built a game where the challenge curve is emergent. Two players running the same station with the same seed can have wildly different experiences depending on whether one of them sells counterfeit stims to an elite and the other plays it clean.

Community reports from the first weeks after the October 28, 2026 launch echo this: players describe their runs getting harder or easier based on decisions, not menu toggles. If you're looking for a single switch labeled "make this easier," you won't find one. What you will find is a set of interlocking systems that behave like a difficulty dial once you understand them.

### The Four Systems That Actually Control Difficulty

| System | What It Controls | Turn It Up (Harder) | Turn It Down (Easier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting profession | Your opening capital, licenses, and risk profile | Fence, organ dealer, or "rock bottom" start | Water merchant or hydroponic farmer |
| Product line | Margin size vs. inspection and raid frequency | Weapons and profitable chemicals | Water, cigarettes, basic produce |
| Faction reputation | Access to exclusive deals vs. attention from Security | Play all sides, sell to elites | Stay neutral, keep a low profile |
| Rent and expansion | Time pressure and cash-flow squeeze | Expand fast, run multiple businesses | Stay small, bank cash reserves |

Treat those four as your real difficulty menu. Most players who complain the game is "too hard" are running a high-margin contraband build with an aggressive expansion plan — that's essentially selecting Nightmare mode without realizing it.

## Your Starting Profession Is the Biggest Difficulty Setting

The game lets you begin as a gunsmith, chemist, water merchant, fence, farmer, or from absolute rock bottom. Each path comes with its own profit curve, its own regulatory headaches, and its own way of ruining you. This is the closest thing Probably Stolen has to a difficulty selection screen — and it's a permanent one, since your profession shapes your early inventory, licenses, and supplier relationships.

| Starting Path | Relative Difficulty | Why | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water merchant | Low | Steady demand, low regulatory heat | First-time shopkeepers learning the loop |
| Hydroponic farmer | Low–Medium | Self-supplied stock, slow but safe scaling | Players who like production chains |
| Chemist | Medium | High margins, addiction liability, guarded recipes | Players comfortable with recipe management |
| Gunsmith | Medium–High | Permits, modifications, and inspections | Players who enjoy paperwork-as-gameplay |
| Fence | High | Stolen goods, raids, constant hiding | Veterans chasing tension |
| Rock bottom | Very High | No capital cushion, no safety net | Masochists and content creators |

A useful rule of thumb from player experience: pick a low-risk profession for your first two rent cycles, learn how inspection and appraisal actually feel, then restart with a fence or rock-bottom run once you know which mistakes are survivable.

## Rent Pressure: The Difficulty Slider Nobody Labels

Rent is the quiet engine behind every tense moment in the game. The core loop is simple — earn enough to buy out your shop before rising rent forces your hand — but the pacing of that climb is what separates a relaxed playthrough from a sweaty one.

Because rent keeps increasing, your margin for error shrinks every cycle. A bad deal in cycle one is annoying. A bad deal in cycle five can mean losing confiscated stock you can't replace. That escalation is the closest the game comes to a built-in difficulty ramp, and it's not optional.

You can, however, influence how hard that ramp bites:

- **Bank a buffer before expanding.** Two cycles of rent in reserve turns a disaster into a setback.
- **Avoid buying machines that don't pay for themselves within one rent cycle.** Every purchase needs to earn its keep.
- **Delay the second business.** Running multiple storefronts multiplies income and multiplies your exposure to raids, inspections, and staffing problems at the same time.
- **Sell slow, safe goods during a crunch.** Water and filtered basics won't make you rich, but they keep the lights on.

### Expansion Timing Cheat Sheet

| Your Situation | Expand? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 1 rent cycle in savings | No | One raid wipes you out |
| 1–2 cycles saved, stable suppliers | Maybe | Test one new product line first |
| 2+ cycles saved, licenses unlocked | Yes | You can absorb a bad quarter |
| Mid-raid investigation ongoing | Absolutely not | Confiscation risk is at its peak |

## Faction Reputation and Risk: The Soft Difficulty Modifiers

If rent is the pace, reputation is the texture. Every customer could be a security fisher gathering evidence, a revolutionary looking for leverage, or a fallen elite testing whether you're useful. Approving, refusing, reporting, or ignoring each sale moves your standing with somebody — and standing cuts both ways.

High reputation buys access and protection. It also paints a target. That trade-off is the single most effective difficulty modifier in the game, because it's entirely player-driven. A shopkeeper who stays boring and neutral will see far fewer raids than one who sells counterfeit luxury goods to the elite and brags about it.

The aug-detection system adds another layer. A rogue AI chip incident left augmented hosts walking among the population, and every faction agrees on the policy — you have the gun to enforce it. Missing the signs, or misreading them, carries its own price. Players who want a harder run can lean into enforcement aggressively; players who want a calmer one can look away more often and accept the reputation hit.

### Risk Systems Compared

| Risk System | Trigger | Consequence | How to Reduce It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security inspections | Weapons, permits, visible stock | Fines, confiscation | Keep documentation clean, hide contraband |
| Raids | Stolen goods, high faction heat | Lost inventory, bribery costs | Limit fence stock, bribe early |
| Product liability | Water, chemicals, cheap cuts | Reputation damage, refunds | Test, filter, and don't over-dilute |
| Aug enforcement | Missing behavioral signs | Faction backlash either way | Learn the tells before acting |
| Mob justice | Repeatedly cheating customers | Permanent consequences | Don't burn the same neighborhood twice |

## Practical Tips for Tuning Difficulty Mid-Run

You can't change the menu, but you can absolutely change how the next ten hours feel. These adjustments work at any point in a save:

1. **Audit your product mix.** If more than a third of your shelf space is contraband, you're running a high-risk build. Swap two slots for legal goods and watch raid frequency drop.
2. **Appraise before you buy.** Rare items need proper evaluation to avoid overpaying, and suppliers routinely overstate value. Misjudging an item means absorbing the cost yourself.
3. **Use the haggle system deliberately.** Every bargain comes with a trade-off. Haggling hard on a desperate customer is profitable and reputation-costly; haggling soft builds goodwill you'll need later.
4. **Track your reputation with all factions, not just your favorite.** Being loved by revolutionaries and hated by Security is not a stable business model.
5. **Keep a hiding spot and a straight face.** Some trades need heavy machinery and permits; others just need concealment and nerve. Know which one you're running.
6. **Slow down during investigations.** The cheapest way to survive a raid is to have nothing incriminating on the shelf when it happens.

## FAQ

**Are there actual difficulty settings in Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim?**
Not in the traditional sense. At launch, the Steam listing shows no Easy/Normal/Hard selector. Instead, the challenge level emerges from your starting profession, product line, faction relationships, and expansion pace. Players who want a gentler run should pick water merchant or farmer and stay away from contraband.

**What's the easiest way to play Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim difficulty settings on a low-stress build?**
Start as a water merchant or hydroponic farmer, keep at least one full rent cycle in savings, avoid selling stolen goods, and delay your second business until you have two cycles banked. This combination minimizes inspections, raids, and cash-flow panic.

**Can I make the game harder without restarting?**
Yes. Shift your stock toward contraband, take aggressive faction stances, stop bribing your way out of trouble, and expand faster than your savings can cover. Community players report that these choices produce a noticeably sharper difficulty curve within a few cycles.

**Do the starting professions really change difficulty that much?**
Considerably. A fence or rock-bottom start removes your financial cushion and puts you on Security's radar from day one, while a farmer start gives you self-supplied stock and a slow, safe scaling path. Treat the profession choice as your real difficulty selection screen.
