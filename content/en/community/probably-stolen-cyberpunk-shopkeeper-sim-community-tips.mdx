## Choose Your Product Line Before You Choose Your Morals

Probably Stolen lets you open as a gunsmith, chemist, water merchant, fence, farmer, or from absolute rock bottom. The game frames this as picking a theme. It is really picking which kind of trouble you are willing to monetize — weapons draw inspections, chemicals draw addiction and recipe theft, water draws liability claims, and contraband draws raids.

Community reports consistently favor the low-drama trades for a first run. Water and hydroponics do not print money the way a lucky weapon sale can, but they teach the production chain and the appraisal habit without inviting Security to your counter every other day. Once you understand how the store's traffic responds to what you stock, the higher-margin lines stop feeling like a gamble and start feeling like a schedule.

| Starter path | Margin ceiling | Recurring headache | Recommended for run one? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water merchant | Low to moderate | Testing, filtering, liability | Yes — safest teaching tool |
| Hydroponic farmer | Moderate, slow ramp | Machine upkeep and supply chains | Yes — pairs well with water |
| Fence | Moderate to high | Raids, mob justice, heat | No — save for run two |
| Chemist | High | Guarded recipes, addiction fallout | No |
| Gunsmith | High | Permits, modifications, inspections | No |
| Rock bottom start | Varies wildly | No cushion for a bad week | Only if you like pain |

The community tips that show up most often about this choice are blunt: the better the margins, the worse the consequences when something goes wrong. A weapon that fails inspection costs you the item *and* the cash you spent acquiring it. A water batch that skips filtration costs you a customer's trust instead. Only one of those is survivable in week one.

## Rent Math: The Only Scoreboard That Matters

Rent increases over time regardless of how well your week went. That single design choice reframes the entire game. You are not trying to maximize profit; you are trying to stay ahead of a number that never stops climbing. Community reports describe the death spiral in identical terms — attractiveness drops, foot traffic drops, revenue drops, and suddenly the only deals big enough to close the gap are the ones most likely to get your stock confiscated.

| Cash-flow rule | What it prevents | When to break it |
|---|---|---|
| Bank a full rent cycle before upgrading machines | Forced fire sales and desperate risk | Almost never |
| Reinvest only surplus after rent is covered | The "great week, broke month" trap | When a licensed upgrade unlocks a new revenue stream |
| Keep one liquid slot for a fast flip | Sitting on dead inventory while rent looms | If a supplier offers a verified rare at a discount |
| Never spend the last of your cash on contraband | Losing everything to one raid | When you have a hiding spot and a bribe ready |
| Track your average weekly revenue, not your best week | Budgeting on a fluke | After three consistent weeks |

The practical takeaway from Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim community tips on this front is unglamorous: treat your best week as an outlier and your worst week as your baseline. Budget against the worst case and the rising rent line becomes a manageable slope instead of a cliff.

## Reading the Counter: Customers, Factions, and Augs

Every customer on the station is potentially a security fisher gathering evidence, a revolutionary hunting leverage, a fallen elite testing whether you are still useful, or someone entirely ordinary buying cigarettes. You can approve a sale, refuse it, report it, or look away — and someone is always taking notes. Reputation buys access and protection, but it also paints a target on your back.

| Customer read | Tell | Smart response |
|---|---|---|
| Security fisher | Asks oddly specific questions about sourcing | Answer plainly, sell nothing incriminating |
| Revolutionary | Wants leverage, not product | Trade information carefully, keep receipts |
| Fallen elite | Condescending, tests your usefulness | Sell counterfeit goods at premium — with caution |
| Ordinary civilian | Straightforward, price-sensitive | Haggle gently, build repeat traffic |
| Aug (AI-controlled host) | Behavior that reads slightly wrong | Use the gun — every faction agrees on the policy |

Community reports suggest the aug check is where most players lose reputation they did not know they had. Missing the signs, or misreading an innocent customer as a host, both carry a price. The safe habit is to watch body language and speech patterns before you watch the price tag. If something feels off, slow down. There is no timed input pressure here, so there is no excuse for a rushed call.

## Item Prep: The Checklist That Saves Margins

Every item you stock carries its own risk profile, and skipping prep work is the second-most-common way shopkeepers bleed cash. Suppliers overstate value, customers push their luck, and contraband frequently arrives with problems you only notice after the deal closes.

| Item type | Required prep | Cost of skipping it |
|---|---|---|
| Cigarette packs | Visual inspection before resale | Selling damaged stock, reputation hit |
| Water | Test and filter every batch | Liability claims and lost regulars |
| Weapons | Permits and modifications | Confiscation during inspection |
| Chemicals | Protect the recipe, control dosage | Addiction fallout and heat |
| Rare goods | Proper appraisal before purchase | Overpaying on a fake |
| Counterfeits | Relabel convincingly | Faction blowback if a client notices |
| Stolen goods | Secure hiding spot before stocking | Raid losses, mob justice |

The community consensus on appraisal is worth repeating: your margin is decided at the moment of purchase, not the moment of sale. Misjudge the item or the person selling it and you absorb the entire cost.

## Contraband, Bribes, and Damage Control

Smuggling is the most profitable and most fragile part of the shop. Hiding stolen goods from authorities works until it does not, and bribing your way out of a bust is a solution that scales badly — the second bribe always costs more than the first. Community reports suggest treating contraband as a separate ledger: fund it from surplus, never from rent money, and always know your exit before the deal closes.

A useful habit is to decide your reporting policy before the customer walks in. Reporting certain characters can have reputation consequences you did not anticipate — early patch notes even adjusted how one specific report affected Black Market standing, which tells you the developers are actively tuning those trade-offs. Pick a lane, stay consistent, and do not flip-flop based on a single tempting offer.

## FAQ

**What are the most reliable Probably Stolen Cyberpunk Shopkeeper Sim community tips for a first run?**
Start with water or hydroponics, bank a full rent cycle before upgrading anything, and appraise every rare item before you buy. Those three habits prevent the majority of early collapses.

**Why do I keep falling behind on rent even after a good week?**
Because rent climbs regardless of performance. Community reports blame budgeting against your best week instead of your average. Track three weeks of revenue and set your spending against the weakest one.

**Is smuggling worth the risk in Probably Stolen?**
Yes, once you have surplus cash and a hiding spot. Fencing stolen goods and selling counterfeits to arrogant elites are among the strongest profit lines, but funding them with rent money turns one raid into a lost run.

**How do I tell an aug from a normal customer?**
Watch behavior rather than appearance — augs look like everyone else until they do not. Community reports describe subtle behavioral tells, and both missing them and falsely accusing a civilian carry consequences, so slow down and observe before you act.
